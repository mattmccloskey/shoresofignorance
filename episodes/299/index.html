Fact Checks

  • Chronic stress shrinks your ability to think long term

    Needs Context

    Michael (11:11) said operating under stress shrinks your planning window, attributing it to the "cerebral cortex." The well-supported version involves the prefrontal cortex: Arnsten (2009) reviews evidence that even mild uncontrollable stress rapidly impairs PFC function, and chronic stress causes PFC dendritic loss tied to impaired working memory and flexible control, shifting control toward more habitual/amygdala-driven responses. Planning is a PFC function, but the specific "window shrinks to the next hour" framing is a popular simplification. Effects reverse when stress abates.

  • Spouses often die soon after a long-married partner

    Verified

    The "widowhood effect" is well documented: mortality rises significantly in the months after a spouse's death, especially the first 3 to 6 months.

  • Median age of COVID death was above average life expectancy

    Needs Context

    True in the UK (ONS median ~82 to 83 vs life expectancy ~81) and several European countries. In the US the median age of COVID death (roughly 75 to 80 depending on period) was close to, and sometimes below, life expectancy (~78.8 pre-pandemic). Point about skew toward the elderly stands.

  • Unarmed Black Americans not more likely to be shot by police; "fire that guy from Harvard"

    Needs Context

    Likely Roland Fryer's 2016 NBER paper (w22399), which found racial disparities in non-lethal force but no racial difference in officer-involved shootings in Houston data. The finding is contested. Fryer was suspended by Harvard in 2019 over sexual harassment findings, not fired for the study, though he and others argued the study made him a target.

  • Carl Jung on people thinking in words vs. pictures

    Needs Context

    Jung wrote about psychological types (thinking, feeling, sensation, intuition), not specifically verbal vs. visual thinkers. The words-vs-pictures distinction traces more to Francis Galton's imagery research and later popular psychology (e.g. Temple Grandin).

  • Newspeak shrinking to ~34 words / no need to speak

    Needs Context

    In 1984, Syme says Newspeak is "the only language in the world whose vocabulary gets smaller every year" and that "orthodoxy means not thinking, not needing to think." There's no 34-word figure; the spirit of Matt's paraphrase is right.

  • Hemingway on Fitzgerald in A Moveable Feast

    Verified

    Hemingway describes Fitzgerald's face tightening "until it was like a death's head" when drinking, and portrays Zelda as jealous of Scott's work and encouraging his drinking. Hemingway's account is famously one-sided.

  • Americans are the fattest people in all of history

    Needs Context

    The US has among the highest obesity rates of any large country (~40% of adults), and current rates are historically unprecedented. Several Pacific Island nations (e.g. Nauru, Tonga) have higher rates.
