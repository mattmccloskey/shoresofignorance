Fact Checks

  • Podcasts were invented by Apple, and 'POD' was the iPod

    Incorrect

    The word 'podcast' was coined by Ben Hammersley in The Guardian on February 12, 2004, as a portmanteau of 'iPod' and 'broadcast.' Apple did not invent the format; it added podcast support to iTunes in June 2005, which is what popularized it. Michael is right that 'pod' pointed at the iPod and 'cast' at broadcasting.

  • Streams in the Desert is a daily devotional, published in 1925, written by a woman who was a missionary in Japan and China

    Verified

    Lettie B. Cowman (published as Mrs. Charles E. Cowman) first issued Streams in the Desert in 1925. She and her husband were missionaries in Japan with the Oriental Missionary Society, which also worked in China and Korea. The book was compiled after Charles Cowman's death in 1924.

  • The April 9 entry in that book says, 'let us take the weights as well as the wings'

    Verified

    The April 9 reading, often titled 'Spiritual Force,' quotes A. B. Simpson: 'Let us thank Him for both, let us take the weights as well as the wings.' Michael first quotes it that way, then later as 'let us accept the weights as well as the wings.' They recorded on Wednesday, April 9, 2025, the day of that entry; the episode was published the next day.

  • Viktor Frankl said that mental health is not a state of tensionlessness, but that you are always in tension between who you are and who you could be

    Verified

    In Man's Search for Meaning Frankl writes that mental health is based on a certain degree of tension, 'the tension between what one has already achieved and what one still ought to accomplish, or the gap between what one is and what one should become,' and he calls the ideal of a tensionless state a dangerous misconception of mental hygiene.

  • In Out of the Silent Planet there is a type of creature called something like the 'idylli,' spirits that appear as a shimmer of light

    Needs Context

    The creatures are the eldila (singular eldil), unembodied intelligences that can appear as a variation of light. They are introduced in Out of the Silent Planet and developed further in Perelandra. Michael flags that he may not have the word right.

  • Entropy was coined by French engineers who were trying to build a perfectly efficient steam engine and discovered that you cannot

    Needs Context

    The Veritasium video Michael is working from does open with Sadi Carnot, a French engineer, and his 1820s study of the steam engine and the impossibility of a lossless heat engine. The word 'entropy' itself was coined later, in 1865, by the German physicist Rudolf Clausius, building on Carnot and Kelvin.

  • In The Cutting Edge, a washed-up hockey player is paired with a young female figure skater, keeps falling because of the toe pick, and they go on to win some award, maybe the Olympics

    Needs Context

    The 1992 film follows Doug Dorsey, a hockey player whose career ends after an eye injury, and Kate Moseley, a pairs skater. She repeatedly yells 'Toe pick!' as he adjusts to figure skates, and they compete at the Olympics, winning silver. Michael is loose on the injury (he guesses knees or age) and on what they win.

  • A Google list of common items invented by NASA includes memory foam, water filters, freeze-drying, scratch-resistant lenses, ear thermometers, invisible braces, camera phones, baby formula, and cochlear implants

    Needs Context

    Some of these are real NASA spinoffs or NASA-related developments, including memory foam, infrared ear thermometers, and scratch-resistant lens coatings. Others are overstated or wrong as NASA inventions: freeze-drying predates the space program, infant formula and cochlear implants were not NASA products, and camera phones were commercial consumer devices, even if CMOS sensor work at JPL is sometimes cited in the background. Popular 'NASA invented this' lists tend to blur that line, and Michael is reading one aloud.
