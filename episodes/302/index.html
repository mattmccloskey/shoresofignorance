About This Episode

Riding together on the way to dinner before recording, we passed a stalled car in a turn lane. We had the same thought about stopping to push it, and we kept on driving.

After a week of missile launchers and prepper panic, that moment stuck with us. Uncle Screwtape wants our malice aimed at neighbors and our kindness at strangers far away, and we wonder why we know what's good and still settle for box wine. We also get into Kreeft on our insanity, David and Bathsheba, a glider club, and Paul's joy in a jail cell.

Fact Checks

  • "Weeks where years happen" quote

    Needs Context

    Matt (0:23) half-remembered it; Michael associates it with bitcoin. The usual form is "There are decades where nothing happens; and there are weeks where decades happen," almost always credited to Lenin. Quote Investigator found no evidence Lenin said or wrote it; the first attribution to him appears in 2001.

  • Avenger missile launchers around Mar-a-Lago

    Verified

    At the Secret Service's request, NORAD and U.S. Northern Command deployed ground-based air defense and counter-drone systems around Mar-a-Lago in mid-October 2026. Two Humvee-mounted AN/TWQ-1 Avengers (Stinger missiles) sit on a barge in Lake Worth Lagoon, with more at Palm Beach's Trump International Airport. A LOCUST counter-drone laser was also spotted by Oct 31. Matt's "Avenger missile launchers" is accurate.

  • Europe told people to stock 3 to 90 days of supplies

    Needs Context

    Most European household guidance is 72 hours (France, Ireland, the Netherlands, Finland, and now the UK). Germany recommends 10 days. The 90-day figure belongs to the Pentagon's standard for military bases, not household guidance. Matt's own correction on air (much of it is old, the UK's is new) is right.

  • Hegseth: bases self-sufficient for 90 days

    Needs Context

    Hegseth unveiled the FORTRESS initiative in his Sept 30, 2026 State of the Force address, promising bases would generate their own power. The 90-day standard came from a Fox News report on Oct 25 citing internal planning documents, with spokesman Sean Parnell quoted. It covers major continental U.S. installations and lists electricity, water, fuel and communications. Food isn't specifically listed, and Snopes notes the 90-day figure hasn't been formally confirmed.

  • Learjet 60 from Austin to the Bahamas crashed

    Verified

    On Nov 1, 2026, a Learjet 60 (N722BD) flying from Austin-Bergstrom to North Eleuthera crashed in Greenacres, Florida, killing all nine aboard, including Austin tech entrepreneur Adam Robinson, his wife Helen Sharp and their two young children (the family had recently moved to Aspen). The crew reported icing and moderate turbulence before a mayday and overspeed call. The NTSB has found evidence of in-flight breakup; cause not yet determined.

  • Is there really a war in Iran?

    Needs Context

    Matt (5:08) wondered whether the "so called war in Iran" is a war, since he hadn't seen pictures. As of Nov 4, 2026 the U.S.-Iran conflict is widely reported: AP reported U.S. strikes on Iran on the eve of the midterms, with fighting around the Strait of Hormuz. Matt's point about how little of it most of us actually see stands.

  • Piper's missionary couple and the retirees' boat

    Needs Context

    The missionaries were two women, not a couple: Ruby Eliason, over 80, and Laura Edwards, a widowed doctor near 80, killed when their brakes failed in Cameroon. The "tragedy" was a Reader's Digest couple, Bob and Penny, who retired at 59 and 51 to Punta Gorda, Florida, to "cruise on their 30-foot trawler, play softball and collect shells." No lake house or cocktails, but Matt's summary of the point is right.

  • Peter Kreeft has written more books than his years

    Verified

    Kreeft was born March 16, 1937 (89) and is still teaching at Boston College in his 62nd year. Word on Fire's Brandon Vogt counts 110 books (National Catholic Register, Aug 2026). He's a philosopher and Catholic apologist more than a theologian by title.

  • Teresa of Ávila's "I want to want to love you"

    Needs Context

    Widely attributed to Teresa of Ávila in sermons and devotional writing, but no primary source in her works was found. Treat it as attributed.

  • The Quran says the crucifixion was made to appear so

    Verified

    Quran 4:157: "they did not kill him, nor did they crucify him, but it was made to appear so to them." Muslim interpreters explain it in different ways (often a substitute); "God deceived everyone" is the Christian interlocutor's argument in the video, not the Muslim reading.

  • Islam offers no assurance of forgiveness

    Needs Context

    Michael (36:37). The Quran stresses God's forgiveness ("Allah forgives all sins," 39:53) and describes deeds weighed on scales (101:6-9). Mainstream Sunni teaching holds that believers live between hope and fear without guaranteed assurance, so "no assurance" is closer than "no forgiveness."

  • Uriah was one of David's mighty men

    Verified

    2 Samuel 23:39 lists Uriah the Hittite among David's mighty men, and 2 Samuel 11 to 12 matches Matt's retelling: the pregnancy, the attempt to send Uriah home, his refusal out of loyalty, his death at the front, and Nathan's parable of the poor man's lamb. Michael's "top general" overstates his rank, and the story is in 2 Samuel, not First Kings.

  • Jesus called himself the real bread and real wine

    Needs Context

    "True bread from heaven" and "the bread of life" are John 6:32-35. The parallel saying is "I am the true vine" (John 15:1), a vine rather than wine; the wine connection comes through Cana and the Last Supper cup.

  • Lewis's heaven and hell line is in The Great Divorce

    Verified

    Matt guessed among The Great Divorce, Mere Christianity and The Weight of Glory. It's The Great Divorce, Chapter 9 ("Meeting George"): the Blessed will say "We have never lived anywhere except in Heaven," and the Lost, "We were always in Hell." And both will speak truly.

  • Glider pilots can soar 8 hours with no engine

    Verified

    Soaring pilots work thermals for flights of many hours, and Texas is one of the best regions for it. Dustin Martin's 2012 world-record hang glider flight from Zapata ran about 11 hours and 475 miles.

  • Most homes lacked electricity until about 100 years ago

    Verified

    Roughly half of U.S. homes had electricity in the mid-1920s and about 68% by 1930. Rural America and most of the world came later.

  • Showers and fridges arrived around 1900

    Needs Context

    Later than Matt said, which helps his point. In 1940 nearly half of U.S. homes lacked complete plumbing, and only about a quarter had mechanical refrigerators; iceboxes were common before that.

  • Paul wrote of surpassing joy from a cave-like cell

    Needs Context

    Philippians 3:8 says "the surpassing worth of knowing Christ" (joy runs through the whole letter). Paul wrote Philippians in custody (1:13), traditionally under house arrest in Rome in rented quarters (Acts 28:16, 30); Ephesus and Caesarea are also proposed. The cave picture is closer to the Philippian inner prison of Acts 16.
