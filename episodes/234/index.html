Fact Checks

  • The book 1776 was written by John McCullough

    Incorrect

    The author is David McCullough. 1776 was published in 2005 and does draw heavily on letters and contemporary accounts, including Washington's. Michael supplies the name in the moment and has the surname right and the first name wrong.

  • Heather Weinstein renamed Václav Havel's greengrocer sign 'the Don't Hurt Me wall' during the 2000s

    Incorrect

    The phrase is associated with Heather Heying, Bret Weinstein's wife, not 'Heather Weinstein,' and with the business signage and yard signs of 2020, not the 2000s. Havel's greengrocer, from The Power of the Powerless, was written in 1978, later than Matt's 'late 60s, early 70s.'

  • About 300,000 enslaved people were brought to America in the transatlantic slave trade, and the trade as a whole moved 18 million

    Needs Context

    The standard figures from the Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade Database are about 12.5 million embarked and about 10.7 million who survived the Middle Passage, of whom roughly 388,000 disembarked in mainland North America. Matt's 'about 300,000' is in the right neighborhood for North America; 18 million is high for the transatlantic trade itself, though larger totals sometimes appear when other African slave trades are folded in.

  • The United States uniquely did not participate in what was the norm of the slave trade, and uniquely tried to root it out

    Incorrect

    The United States did participate in the transatlantic trade until Congress banned the importation of enslaved people in 1808, and it maintained a large domestic system of slavery until the Civil War and the Thirteenth Amendment. The North American share of the transatlantic trade was relatively small, and Britain had already banned the trade in 1807 and emancipated the enslaved in its empire in the 1830s. 'Uniquely did not participate' is not right.

  • John McElroy said Serena Williams would lose to the 200th-ranked men's player, and Serena later admitted that men play a different game

    Needs Context

    The comment is John McEnroe's, from a June 2017 NPR interview, and the number he used was 'like 700 in the world,' not 200. He also called her the best female player ever. Serena replied that the ranking claim was not factually based. She has, in other interviews, described men's and women's tennis as different games, with men hitting harder and moving faster, which is the part Michael is remembering.

  • The Boy Scouts of America dropped 'boys' and became just Scouts of America

    Needs Context

    In May 2024 the Boy Scouts of America announced it would rebrand as Scouting America, a change that took effect in 2025. Girls had already been admitted to the Scouts BSA program in 2019. Matt has the direction of the change right and the exact new name slightly off.

  • The Black Lives Matter organization's website listed disruption of the nuclear family among its goals

    Verified

    The Movement for Black Lives / BLM Global Network 'What We Believe' page, later taken down, included the line about disrupting the Western-prescribed nuclear family structure requirement by supporting each other as extended families and villages. That language was widely circulated in 2020 and is what Matt is pointing to.

  • Ford and Chevy are made in Mexico, and Tesla is made in America and employs 80,000 U.S. citizens

    Needs Context

    Tesla builds vehicles in Fremont and Austin as well as in Shanghai and Berlin, so 'made in America' is only partly right. Ford and Chevrolet also manufacture extensively in the United States, as well as in Mexico and Canada. Tesla's global headcount in this period was well above 80,000; the U.S. figure Michael cites is a rough, unattributed number, offered in March 2025 amid protests over Elon Musk and DOGE.

  • Just because you cannot produce a definition for a word does not mean you do not know what it means, a point Matt attributes to Bret Weinstein

    Needs Context

    This is a standard point in linguistics and ordinary-language philosophy, going back at least to the observation that fluent speakers often cannot define the words they use correctly. Matt is recalling Bret making the point; whether that is the original source is another matter, and he presents it as something he has heard Bret say.
