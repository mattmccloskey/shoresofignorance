About This Episode

We're more attuned to the thorn than to the sunshine, so gratitude has to be chosen. That's part of why we still sit down at a Thanksgiving table with people we might disagree with, and hitch ourselves to a shared fate.

The trouble is how often we commune with things that aren't real: a lie we have to keep holding up, a screen, sex without intent. Naming is the way back, even if you're wrong.

Cheers y'all 🍻

Fact Checks

  • Short Circuit was released in 1986

    Verified

    Matt (1:01) looks it up mid-conversation and says 1986. The film was released in the U.S. on May 9, 1986.

  • Kids' faces appeared on milk cartons in the 1980s

    Verified

    Matt (15:11) remembers "kids faces on milk cartons" from childhood. Anderson Erickson Dairy in Des Moines began printing missing-children photos in September 1984, and the National Child Safety Council took the program nationwide that December. It ran into the mid-1990s.

  • "It's 10 p.m. Do you know where your children are?" was a real TV PSA

    Verified

    Matt (15:56) says it "was an actual thing on the TV every night." The line was used as a late-evening PSA and news-open on American television from the late 1960s through the late 1980s, most famously on WNEW-TV in New York.

  • Getting kidnapped "doesn't happen so much these days"

    Needs Context

    Matt (15:11) asks whether kidnapping still happens and says "you don't really hear of that happening so much these days," after remembering milk cartons and 1980s movies. Stereotypical stranger abductions were always rare. NISMART estimates were about 115 such cases in 1997 and about 105 in 2011, essentially unchanged, though a much higher share of children were recovered alive in 2011. The 1980s fear was larger than the underlying rate, then and now.

  • People who live together before marriage have higher divorce rates

    Needs Context

    Matt (56:52) says "people who live together before they get married have higher divorce rates than people who don't." That is the classic "cohabitation effect," and some recent work still finds a remaining gap (Kerrigan and Bailey, 2021, 31.4% vs 25.9% in raw NSFG figures, about 4.6 points after controls). Other research argues the association has weakened or vanished for more recent cohorts, or is partly explained by age at first coresidence rather than cohabitation itself. The old finding is not simply false, but it is not settled either.

  • A woman in the 1970s lived with a dolphin, gave dolphins LSD, and had sex with it

    Needs Context

    Matt (63:46) places the story in the 1970s, says researchers were giving dolphins LSD to teach them to talk, flooded a lab so a woman could live with a young male dolphin, and that she "started letting the dolphin have sex." The experiment was earlier, in 1965-66, at John Lilly's NASA-funded lab on St. Thomas. Margaret Howe Lovatt did live with Peter in a flooded upper floor of the Dolphin House, and she later described sexually relieving him so the work could continue. Lilly used LSD on dolphins; Lovatt has said she would not let him give it to Peter, though a April 19, 1965 recording in Lilly's papers is labeled as Peter after LSD.

  • Jesus holding reality in being is in James or Colossians

    Needs Context

    Matt (41:14) says "I think in James, I don't know where it says. Or Colossians, maybe," that Jesus, the logos who spoke reality into being, still holds it in being. The line is Colossians 1:17, "in him all things hold together." Hebrews 1:3 is a close parallel. It is not in James.

  • The demons said "Jesus I know, and Paul I know, but who are you?"

    Verified

    Michael (104:43) recounts the scene in Acts where a spirit says, "I know Paul, I know Jesus, but I don't know you." Acts 19:15: "Jesus I know, and Paul I recognize, but who are you?" The seven sons of Sceva had been using the name without knowing the person.

  • Judas betrayed and Peter denied Jesus on the same night

    Verified

    Matt (79:07) says that on the same night Judas betrays Jesus, Peter also betrays him, and that the difference is what they did after: "Peter runs and throws himself at the feet of Jesus. Judas hangs himself." Judas's betrayal and Peter's denials are both on the night of the arrest (Matthew 26). Judas hangs himself in Matthew 27:3-5. Peter's restoration, closer to "throws himself at the feet of Jesus," is after the resurrection, in John 21:15-19, not immediately that night.

  • Jordan Peterson describes sin in Genesis 4:7 as a sexually aroused predator

    Verified

    Matt (89:43) says he is taking from Peterson the reading that God tells Cain "sin crouched at your door like a sexually aroused animal, and you invited it in." Michael immediately says "it doesn't say that." Peterson, in Biblical Series V, does liken the verse to a "sexually aroused, cat predator" whose desire is for you. The sexual coloring comes from teshuqah, the same "desire" word used of Eve in Genesis 3:16, not from a literal phrase in the verse. Matt correctly flags it as Peterson's interpretation.

  • The Vampire Diaries has 21 seasons

    Needs Context

    Matt (73:00), talking about using entertainment to avoid the knowledge that you are not a good person, says "just give me my Instagram reels, give me my, you know, my 21 seasons of whatever, Vampire Diaries or whatever." The Vampire Diaries ran 8 seasons, from 2009 to 2017. He is clearly speaking loosely.
