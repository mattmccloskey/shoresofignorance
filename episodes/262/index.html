Fact Checks

  • Osama bin Laden's pre-9/11 open letter circulated with Gen Z about six months ago

    Incorrect

    As of January 2026 both details were off. Bin Laden's 'Letter to America' was written in 2002, about a year after the September 11 attacks, and it resurfaced on TikTok in November 2023, more than two years before this recording. NPR found fewer than 300 tagged videos with about 2 million views before coverage on X pushed the total to about 13 million.

  • Nick Shirley visited Minnesota day cares that appeared empty, and one sign read 'Learing Center'

    Verified

    Shirley's video, posted in late December 2025, featured Quality Learning Center in Minneapolis, whose sign read 'Quality Learing Center.' The center's manager said Shirley came outside its operating hours, and state regulators said recent inspections had not found evidence of fraud, while state and federal investigations into wider Minnesota program fraud continued.

  • The average taxpayer pays about $13,000 a year in taxes

    Verified

    For tax year 2022, Tax Foundation's analysis of IRS data puts average federal income tax at about $13,890 per return. Michael's math that a billion dollars equals roughly 77,000 taxpayers at $13,000 each is correct.

  • One Minnesota day care center took the equivalent of about 70,000 people's taxes

    Incorrect

    At $13,000 each, 70,000 taxpayers would be about $910 million. Figures cited by CNN put Quality Learning Center at $1.9 million from the state Child Care Assistance Program in 2025 and nearly $10 million since 2019. The billions under discussion in December 2025 referred to prosecutors' estimate that $9 billion or more may have been stolen across 14 state-run programs since 2018, not to a single center.

  • California spent $24 billion on homelessness and homelessness only increased

    Verified

    An April 2024 California State Auditor report found the state allocated nearly $24 billion for homelessness and housing from fiscal 2018-19 through 2022-23, while the homeless population rose to more than 181,000 in 2023 and the state did not consistently track whether its programs worked.

  • About half of tax dollars go to fraud, and ending it would let us cancel income tax for everyone earning under $500,000

    Incorrect

    GAO's 2024 estimate puts federal fraud losses at $233 billion to $521 billion a year, about 3 to 7 percent of federal obligations, not half. Individual income taxes brought in about $2.1 trillion in 2022, and filers outside the top 1 percent paid roughly 60 percent of that, so even the high fraud estimate would cover well under half of what earners below $500,000 pay.

  • Nobody paid taxes before 1913

    Incorrect

    The Sixteenth Amendment (1913) made a permanent federal income tax possible, but before that the federal government ran on tariffs and excise taxes, states and towns levied property taxes, and Congress collected an income tax from 1861 to 1872 to pay for the Civil War.

  • The Child Vaccine Safety Act of 1986 gave vaccine manufacturers immunity from liability

    Needs Context

    The law is the National Childhood Vaccine Injury Act of 1986. It created the Vaccine Injury Compensation Program, funded by an excise tax on each dose, and shields manufacturers from civil liability for unavoidable side effects of properly made and labeled vaccines, which the Supreme Court in Bruesewitz v. Wyeth (2011) held covers design-defect claims. Manufacturers can still be sued for manufacturing defects, failure to warn, fraud and similar misconduct, and the act also set up vaccine safety reporting.

  • Looking deeper, all the evidence shows vaccines do more harm than good

    Incorrect

    As of January 2026 the large body of evidence reviewed by the WHO, the National Academies and most medical bodies finds that routine childhood vaccines prevent far more harm than they cause. A 2024 CDC analysis estimated that vaccinating children born from 1994 to 2023 would prevent about 508 million illnesses and 1.1 million deaths. Federal vaccine policy was being reshaped in 2025 under HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., but that is a policy change, not new evidence that vaccines do net harm.

  • RFK Jr. and Tulsi Gabbard were pushed out by the left

    Verified

    Both are former Democrats. Gabbard left the party in October 2022, and Kennedy dropped his Democratic primary run in October 2023 to run as an independent. By the time of this recording both served in the Trump administration, Kennedy as HHS Secretary and Gabbard as Director of National Intelligence.

  • Hillary Clinton called Trump supporters deplorables

    Verified

    At a September 9, 2016 fundraiser Clinton said half of Trump's supporters belonged in a 'basket of deplorables.'

  • Trump is a free-market, free-trade president rather than an isolationist

    Needs Context

    Trump calls his trade policy fair trade, but in 2025 he imposed the broadest US tariffs in decades, including the April 2 'Liberation Day' tariffs, which most economists describe as the opposite of free trade.

  • C.S. Lewis said schoolchildren complain about grammar because they can't imagine the joy of reading the great poets

    Verified

    From 'The Weight of Glory' (1941): 'The schoolboy beginning Greek grammar cannot look forward to his adult enjoyment of Sophocles as a lover looks forward to marriage.' Matt's paraphrase captures the point.

  • Hanuman can open his rib cage to expose his heart in times of peace

    Needs Context

    Hanuman is the monkey god of the Ramayana tradition. In a popular later story he tears open his chest to show Rama and Sita in his heart, a sign of devotion. The peace and war version Matt learned from his therapist is a reading of that image, not the traditional story itself.

  • The purposes of a man's heart are deep waters, but a man of wisdom draws them out

    Verified

    Proverbs 20:5. The ESV reads 'The purpose in a man's heart is like deep water, but a man of understanding will draw it out.'

  • Jesus says the way you judge others is the way you will be judged

    Verified

    Matthew 7:2: 'For with the judgment you pronounce you will be judged.'

  • Dan Bilzerian got rich playing poker and later said promiscuity is empty and he wants one woman

    Needs Context

    In March 2024 Bilzerian said he now prefers monogamy and called it a more natural state. He credits high-stakes poker for his wealth, but that is disputed, and he also received a family trust from his father, the financier Paul Bilzerian.

  • Genghis Khan is estimated to have had about 2,000 children

    Needs Context

    There is no reliable count. Records name nine children with his chief wife Börte, plus others by other wives and concubines. The famous figure is from a 2003 study estimating that about 16 million men, around 0.5 percent of men worldwide, carry a Y-chromosome lineage linked to him, and later ancient-DNA work has questioned that link.

  • In a polygamous culture most men end up with no partner, so enforced monogamy benefits nearly everyone

    Verified

    Henrich, Boyd and Richerson's 2012 paper 'The puzzle of monogamous marriage' argues that normative monogamy reduces the pool of unmarried low-status men and with it crime and competition, the argument Matt heard from Bret Weinstein and Heather Heying.

  • C.S. Lewis's memoir is called Surprised by Joy

    Verified

    Surprised by Joy: The Shape of My Early Life was published in 1955.

  • In The Matrix, Cypher wanted to go back

    Verified

    Cypher betrays the crew in exchange for being reinserted into the Matrix with no memory, saying 'ignorance is bliss.'
