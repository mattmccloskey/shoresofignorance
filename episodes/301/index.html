About This Episode

Michael sent Matt a song he loved, with a music video that looked completely real, so Matt went looking for the singer and found an Instagram account for someone who doesn't exist.

That got us asking what happens when you can't tell if anyone is behind what you see. Matt worries about a world quietly filling with fakes, while Michael sees a new frontier for honest storytelling. We also get into AI becoming SI, why intent makes art beautiful, talking to the dead, and why analog still matters.

Cheers y'all 🍻

Fact Checks

  • Trump ordered agencies to say "SI" instead of "AI"

    Verified

    Executive order of Sep 29, 2026. Agencies use "Super Intelligence" and "SI" in new official communications. Statutes, contracts and historical documents are untouched. The State Department rolled it out in an Oct 2026 cable. PolitiFact notes the industry already uses "superintelligence" for something that doesn't exist yet.

  • Austin renamed Robert E. Lee Boulevard to "AZ Morton Boulevard"

    Needs Context

    It was Robert E. Lee Road, renamed Azie Morton Road by the Austin City Council on Apr 26, 2018 (effective May 7, 2018), honoring Azie Taylor Morton. Jeff Davis Avenue became William Holland Avenue at the same meeting. The renaming happened; the street type and spelling were off.

    Needs Context

  • Pornography drove the early web

    Needs Context

    A common claim with some truth. Adult sites were early adopters of online payments, streaming video and other tech. Historians dispute that porn was the main driver of internet adoption. Matt said "100% true"; that overstates it.

  • The atomic bomb came before nuclear power

    Verified

    Hiroshima and Nagasaki were August 1945. The first electricity from a nuclear reactor came from EBR-I in Idaho in December 1951.

  • Black Mirror did an AI version of a dead loved one

  • Eric Weinstein interviewed Riley Reid

    Verified

  • Edison's first recording machine cut into a wax cylinder

    Incorrect

    Edison's 1877 phonograph recorded onto tinfoil wrapped around a cylinder. Wax cylinders arrived in the late 1880s (Bell and Tainter's graphophone, then Edison's improved phonograph). The mechanism Matt describes, a horn vibrating a stylus that cuts a groove, is right.

  • Vinyl is a direct physical imprint of the performance

    Needs Context

    True for all-analog chains (tape to lacquer, or direct-to-disc). Many modern vinyl releases are cut from digital masters, so the groove is often a copy of a digital file.

  • Digital audio is "connect the dots"

    Needs Context

    Digital audio samples the waveform at fixed points. By the Nyquist–Shannon theorem, a sufficiently sampled, band-limited signal can be reconstructed exactly, not approximated by joining dots. Matt's larger point, that it is a reconstruction rather than a continuous physical trace, stands.

  • Seeing a Picasso at the Louvre

    Needs Context

    The Louvre's painting collection mostly ends in the mid-1800s. In Paris, Picassos hang at the Musée Picasso and the Centre Pompidou.
