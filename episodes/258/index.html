About This Episode

It’s easy to see when something holy or sacred is violated, it’s harder to say what holiness and the sacred are. An AI representation of dead relatives leads to a conversation about specifying and naming that which is ultimately sacred and good.

Fact Checks

  • A company advertised an AI avatar of a dead loved one, showing a pregnant woman talking to her dead mother and the child growing up with the AI grandma

    Verified

    The app is 2wai, co-founded by former Disney Channel actor Calum Worthy. His ad, posted on X in November 2025, showed exactly that arc and ended with the mother being filmed for three minutes to create the avatar. Forbes reported it had passed 22 million views by November 14, 2025.

  • Bret Weinstein posted 'I'm not a religious person, but this is unholy. That's the only term that captures the depravity of it.'

    Verified

    Weinstein posted those words on X on November 14, 2025, when sharing the 2wai ad.

  • Bret and Heather discussed it on DarkHorse in episode 300 or 301

    Verified

    It was 'Raising the Dead,' their 301st Evolutionary Lens livestream, released November 15, 2025, which discusses 2wai, grief and mourning.

  • Shores of Ignorance has about 13,000 hours of content

    Incorrect

    As of November 2025 the public feed had about 237 episodes totaling roughly 290 hours, a few percent of 13,000. Matt was unsure of the number when he said it.

  • Moses took off his shoes at the burning bush because he was on holy ground

    Verified

    Exodus 3:5: 'Do not come near; take your sandals off your feet, for the place on which you are standing is holy ground.'

  • The vision of angels circling God saying holy, holy, holy is in Ezekiel

    Incorrect

    The cry of 'Holy, holy, holy' comes from the seraphim in Isaiah 6:3 and is echoed by the four living creatures in Revelation 4:8. Ezekiel 1 describes living creatures around God's throne but does not include that cry.

  • God showed Moses his back from a cleft in the rock

    Verified

    Exodus 33:21-23. God puts Moses in a cleft of the rock, covers him with his hand as he passes by, and lets Moses see his back but not his face.

  • The high priest entered the Holy of Holies once a year with a rope tied to him so his body could be dragged out

    Needs Context

    The once-a-year entry on the Day of Atonement is in Leviticus 16. The rope is a later legend. It does not appear in the Bible, the Mishnah, the Talmud or Josephus, and its earliest known source is the medieval Zohar.

  • When Satan tempts Jesus after 40 days in the desert he keeps saying 'if you are who you say you are'

    Verified

    In Matthew 4 and Luke 4 the devil twice opens with 'If you are the Son of God.' Matt's paraphrase captures it, and he corrected 40 years to 40 days.

  • Peter denied Christ and then went on to start the church as we know it, the Catholic Church

    Church Tradition

    Peter's three denials are in all four gospels and Acts shows him leading the early church. The Catholic Church holds that Peter was the first bishop of Rome and the first pope, which is tradition rather than something the New Testament states.

  • David murdered Bathsheba's husband to take her as his wife, and their child became Solomon

    Needs Context

    2 Samuel 11 tells how David had Uriah killed in battle and married Bathsheba. Their first son died in infancy (2 Samuel 12). Solomon was a later son, and 1 Kings 3:12 promises him wisdom like no one before or after him.

  • Peter Singer says the very young and very old are not persons, so it is ethical to kill them

    Needs Context

    In Practical Ethics Singer defines a person as a rational, self-aware being with a sense of its own past and future. He argues newborns are not yet persons and that ending the life of a severely disabled infant can be permissible with the parents' agreement, and he supports voluntary euthanasia. He does not say the elderly in general are not persons, and his definition is about self-awareness, not the capacity for pleasure in relationships.

  • A founding father, perhaps Benjamin Franklin, said it is better that a thousand guilty men go free than one innocent man be convicted

    Needs Context

    The thousand version is from the medieval Jewish scholar Maimonides. Franklin wrote in 1785 that it is better a hundred guilty persons should escape than that one innocent person should suffer, and William Blackstone's 1760s formulation used ten.

  • Jesus said do not judge lest you be judged

    Verified

    Matthew 7:1-2: 'Judge not, that you be not judged. For with the judgment you pronounce you will be judged.'

  • The Pharisees brought a prostitute to Jesus to be stoned

    Incorrect

    In John 8:3-11 the scribes and Pharisees bring a woman caught in adultery, not a prostitute. Jesus answers, 'Let him who is without sin among you be the first to throw a stone at her.'

  • Paul writes in Romans that what he wants to do he does not do, and what he hates he does

    Verified

    Romans 7:15: 'For I do not understand my own actions. For I do not do what I want, but I do the very thing I hate.'

  • Jordan Peterson says every ideal becomes a judge

    Verified

    Peterson has said 'every ideal is a judge' in his Maps of Meaning lectures and elsewhere, meaning the gap between you and an ideal is experienced as judgment.

  • Peterson did a gospel series with a panel of about eight people including atheists, Jews and priests

    Verified

    Peterson's The Gospels series launched on DailyWire+ on December 1, 2024, with a panel of Jonathan Pageau, Catholic Bishop Robert Barron, the Jewish writer Dennis Prager, the non-theist cognitive scientist John Vervaeke, Gregg Hurwitz, James Orr, Douglas Hedley and Stephen Blackwood.

  • Judas hanged himself, while Peter threw himself at Jesus's feet and apologized

    Needs Context

    Matthew 27:5 says Judas hanged himself. After his denials Peter 'went out and wept bitterly' (Luke 22:62). The gospels do not describe him falling at Jesus's feet to apologize, but in John 21 he jumps into the sea to reach the risen Jesus, who restores him with three questions of 'Do you love me?'

  • Derek Chauvin was convicted in the court of public opinion

    Needs Context

    Chauvin was also convicted in a court of law. In April 2021 a Hennepin County jury found him guilty of second-degree murder, third-degree murder and manslaughter in the death of George Floyd, and he was sentenced to 22.5 years. He later pleaded guilty to federal civil rights charges.

  • In Good Will Hunting, Robin Williams hugs Matt Damon and tells him it's not your fault

    Verified

    The scene is one of the film's best known, and Williams won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor for the role.

  • In Narnia, Lucy asks Tumnus whether Aslan is safe and is told he isn't safe but he's good

    Needs Context

    In The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe it is Susan who asks whether Aslan is safe and Mr. Beaver who answers: 'Who said anything about safe? 'Course he isn't safe. But he's good. He's the King, I tell you.' Lucy follows up, and it happens before the children meet Aslan.

  • After 40 years in the desert God told Moses to draw a line in the sand and kill everyone who didn't cross, women, children and animals

    Incorrect

    The story is the golden calf in Exodus 32, at Mount Sinai early in the wandering, not after 40 years. Moses stands at the camp gate and says 'Who is on the Lord's side? Come to me.' The Levites rally to him and kill about three thousand men. There is no line in the sand and the text does not mention killing women, children or animals. The snakes are a later story in Numbers 21.

  • Carl Sagan gave a speech about the pale blue dot

    Verified

    Voyager 1 took the Pale Blue Dot photo in 1990, and Sagan's reflection on it appears in his 1994 book Pale Blue Dot and in talks he gave that year.
