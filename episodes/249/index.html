Fact Checks

  • Isaac Asimov wrote both Foundation and I, Robot, and Foundation is a TV series on Apple

    Verified

    Asimov wrote the Foundation novels and the I, Robot story collection. Apple TV+ began adapting Foundation in 2021. The 2004 Will Smith film I, Robot is a loose adaptation rather than a straight rendering of the book.

  • Computers have surpassed human chess players

    Verified

    IBM's Deep Blue beat Garry Kasparov in 1997, and today's engines and neural-network programs are far stronger than any human. Michael's 'you can't not be beaten' is right about top-level play.

  • Asimov's Three Laws of Robotics are: do not harm humans (or allow harm), obey orders, and protect yourself, in that priority

    Verified

    Michael's ranking matches Asimov: (1) a robot may not injure a human or, through inaction, allow a human to come to harm; (2) it must obey orders unless that conflicts with the First Law; (3) it must protect its own existence unless that conflicts with the First or Second Law.

  • Silicon chips are made from sand, not from 'metal computers'

    Verified

    Semiconductor chips are fabricated from silicon, which is refined from silica sand. Metal is used in packaging and interconnects, but the computation happens in silicon, as Matt says.

  • Jensen Huang, the founder of Nvidia, says 'welcome to the suck'

    Needs Context

    Huang has talked about wishing students 'ample doses of pain and suffering' as formative. 'Welcome to the suck' is a military phrase more often associated with people like Jocko Willink, which is where Matt's ear went. Michael was loosely remembering the spirit of Huang's remarks.

  • An AI generated a large number of new amino acids and estimated their function

    Needs Context

    There is real work on generative protein and peptide design, including some models that venture beyond the 20 canonical amino acids. Matt is likely blending that with the more common story of AI designing novel protein sequences from known amino acids. Treat the 'new amino acids' wording as impressionistic.

  • Around early September 2025, Strategy (formerly MicroStrategy) was expected to be added to the S&P 500 on a Friday, and articles saying September 5th had the date wrong because that was the first Friday, not the second

    Needs Context

    As of this recording on the night of September 3, 2025, inclusion was still a rumor. S&P announced the September rebalance on Friday, September 5 (the first Friday, as the articles said), effective September 22. Michael's calendar math was right; the 'second Friday' expectation was not. Strategy was not added; Robinhood, AppLovin, and EMCOR were.

  • If you go back eight generations, you have 4,000 people in your direct lineage

    Incorrect

    Ignoring pedigree collapse, eight generations of ancestors are 2+4+8+16+32+64+128+256=510 people, with 256 in the eighth generation alone. The count reaches about 4,000 at a single generation only around the 12th (2^12=4,096), or cumulatively around the 11th (4,094). Matt flagged it as a stat he was recalling, and the figure is off.

  • Infants can die without enough physical touch

    Verified

    René Spitz's mid-century 'hospitalism' studies, and later failure-to-thrive and attachment research, found that infants deprived of consistent holding and responsive contact can fail to thrive and sometimes die even when food and hygiene are provided. Matt's 'you will die' is a strong wording of a real finding.

  • Twins separated at birth often share striking later interests and habits

    Needs Context

    The Minnesota Study of Twins Reared Apart documented many unexpected similarities in separated identical twins. Popular retellings sometimes over-egg coincidences, so the phenomenon is real but easy to overstate.

  • The U.S. Constitution says all men are created equal and that they are endowed by their Creator with certain rights

    Needs Context

    Those phrases are from the Declaration of Independence (1776), not the Constitution. Michael reaches for 'the Constitution' twice; the wording he wants is Jefferson's, and Matt's 'endowed by their Creator' cue is the right line from the wrong document.

  • The Bible says he who will save his life will lose it

    Verified

    Matthew 16:25, Mark 8:35, and Luke 9:24 all carry this teaching. Matt uses it as a paradox about pride and pretending.

  • Nietzsche wrote about the death of God

    Verified

    The line appears in The Gay Science and is developed in Thus Spoke Zarathustra. Matt uses it for the cultural demotion of the divine to something as dismissible as astrology.

  • The Dunning–Kruger effect is that beginners report high skill, then confidence crashes as real skill grows, then confidence returns toward mastery

    Needs Context

    The original Dunning–Kruger finding is that the least skilled overestimate themselves. The popular 'peak of Mount Stupid, valley of despair, slope of enlightenment' curve Matt is using is a later popularization that mixes DK with other competence curves. Directionally familiar, not a tight restatement of the paper.
