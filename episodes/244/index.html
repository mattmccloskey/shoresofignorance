Fact Checks

  • The podcast started in 2018, and it took seven years to book Jeanenne

    Verified

    Shores of Ignorance launched in 2018, and this episode was published on July 3, 2025, after a Wednesday-night recording. Seven years is right.

  • Jeanenne and Mike married in 1981, had been married about seven years when they moved toward the ranch, and she had just turned 30

    Verified

    Those dates hang together: a 1981 wedding puts 1988 at seven years, and Jeanenne places the ranch years around 1988 to 1990, when Matt was eight, Katie four, and Andrew two. Her 'just turned 30' fits that window.

  • They moved out to a 40-acre ranch outside Boerne, Texas, after the late-1980s market crash, having been evicted when the rental market was almost entirely full

    Needs Context

    Boerne, pronounced 'Bernie,' is the Hill Country city just north of San Antonio, which is clearly the place they mean. The 1987 stock-market crash and the Texas real-estate and savings-and-loan collapse did scramble Austin housing in the late 1980s. Jeanenne's picture of a seller's market in which wealthy owners downsized and rentals were 99.9 percent occupied is a family memory of a messy local market, not a statistic, and she hedges the year as '1988, when was it, the late 80s.'

  • Jesus said, what does it profit a man to gain the whole world but lose his soul

    Verified

    The line is Jesus' in Mark 8:36 and Matthew 16:26. Jeanenne is quoting it from memory, and the wording is close enough.

  • Gollum worshiped the precious, lost himself, and could not refer to himself as 'I,' only as 'we'

    Verified

    That is how Tolkien writes him: the Ring unmakes his name and his person, and he speaks of himself as 'we' and 'us.' Jeanenne is using the story exactly.

  • God defines himself as I AM

    Verified

    Exodus 3:14, which she is invoking when she says human beings reflect that by saying 'I.'

  • In Ephesians, God says we are his workmanship and uses the word poema, which is a poem

    Verified

    Ephesians 2:10 calls believers God's poiēma, 'workmanship' or 'what he has made.' The Greek word is the source of 'poem,' and reading it as 'we are God's poems' is a long-standing pastoral gloss, which is how Jeanenne is using it.

  • Genesis says God created us male and female, and it takes both together to image God

    Verified

    Genesis 1:27 is the verse. The further claim that male and female together image God is a standard reading of that text, not an extra verse.

  • The heart is deceitful above all else

    Verified

    Jeremiah 17:9, which Jeanenne quotes in almost those words.

  • The younger generation isn't having sex

    Needs Context

    Surveys in the 2010s and 2020s do show declines in sexual activity among teenagers and young adults in the United States. 'Isn't having sex' overstates a real trend as an absence.

  • There are billions of therapists, and they are going to do AI therapy

    Incorrect

    There are millions of mental-health workers worldwide, not billions. The remark is hyperbolic. AI therapy tools were already on the market by the July 2025 recording, so that part of the aside was fair as a direction of travel.

  • Irvin Yalom is 90-something and just came out with The Hour of the Heart, probably his last book, and he is an atheist

    Verified

    Yalom was born in June 1931, so he was 94 at the recording. Hour of the Heart, written with his son Benjamin and published in December 2024, was in fact his latest book then. He is a secular existential psychiatrist and has long described himself as a nonbeliever, which is the contrast Jeanenne is drawing with Kreeft.

  • Peter Kreeft just wrote a book on the heart, is almost 90, and has written something like 80 books

    Needs Context

    Kreeft was born in 1937, so he was 87 or 88 in July 2025, and bibliographies often put his output at 80 books or more. His most direct book on the heart, Wisdom of the Heart, was published in 2020, so 'just wrote' is loose; The Mystery of Joy, from January 2025, is more recent but is a book about joy rather than a treatise titled on the heart.

  • T.S. Eliot said old men should be explorers

    Needs Context

    In East Coker, the second of the Four Quartets, Eliot writes, 'Old men ought to be explorers.' Matt has should for ought, and he is right that the poem turns the exploring inward, onto another plane.

  • Einstein, like Van Gogh and many philosophers, did not see the reaping benefits of his work in his lifetime

    Incorrect

    The larger point stands for Van Gogh and for some of the philosophers they have in mind. It does not stand for Einstein, who was world-famous after 1919 and received the Nobel Prize in 1921, decades before his death.
