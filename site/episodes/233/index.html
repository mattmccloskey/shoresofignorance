Fact Checks

  • Autophagy is the process by which the body cleans up dead or damaged cells, and that process goes up 300% at the 36-hour mark of a fast

    Needs Context

    Autophagy is the cell's recycling of damaged or unneeded components, and it is associated with fasting. The specific 'up to 300% at 36 hours' figure circulates on popular fasting timelines rather than in a clear human study. Human muscle and blood work after 24 to 36 hours of fasting shows mixed, often modest changes in autophagy markers, and researchers caution against treating those blog timelines as settled physiology. Matt is tentative about the timing, first saying 48 hours, then 36. As of the March 19, 2025 recording.

  • The digestive system takes 8 to 12 hours to complete its work, after which it goes dormant

    Needs Context

    A mixed meal often takes on the order of several hours to leave the stomach and small intestine, and a range of 8 to 12 hours is a fair ballpark for that transit. The gut does not simply go dormant afterward: in the fasting state it continues migrating motor complex activity. Matt's larger point, that modern Western eating rarely leaves a long empty window, still stands.

  • Taylor Sheridan wrote Yellowstone, the prequels 1883 and 1923, and Landman; 1883 is the story of the Oregon Trail, and 1923 is the story of settling Montana

    Verified

    Sheridan created Yellowstone, which starred Kevin Costner, then the prequels 1883 and 1923, and later Landman. 1883 follows the Dutton family's wagon-train journey west along the Oregon Trail corridor; 1923 is set on the already established Montana ranch. Matt briefly guesses Ohio, then corrects himself.

  • In The Lord of the Rings, the company is crossing into a last Elven holdout in the East called Nath; they want to blindfold Gimli, Aragorn has everyone blindfolded, and under Haldir's lead Frodo notices his other senses heighten

    Needs Context

    The scene is the crossing into Lothlórien, specifically the Naith of Lórien, which is what 'Nath' is reaching for. The Elves do want to blindfold Gimli, Aragorn insists that the whole company be blindfolded, and Haldir leads them. Frodo's account of trust, the rope, and heightened hearing, smell, and the feel of the grass is right in spirit. Lothlórien is east of the Misty Mountains, but it is not really the last Elven holdout 'in the East' — Woodland Realm Elves still live farther east in Mirkwood.

  • Radiolab in the early 2000s introduced proprioception and interviewed a man who had lost that sense and literally cannot move his body anywhere, and cannot see

    Needs Context

    Radiolab's 'Where Am I?' episode, which includes Ian Waterman's loss of proprioception, was released on May 5, 2006, so 'early 2000s' is close. Waterman can move, but only by constantly watching his limbs; he did not lose his sight, and vision is what makes movement possible for him. Matt has the condition right and that last detail backwards.

  • Jesus said that the Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath

    Verified

    Mark 2:27: 'The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath.' Matt uses it as the rule for whether a discipline is still serving the person who keeps it.

  • Viktor Frankl said that the space between stimulus and response is a man's freedom

    Incorrect

    The wording is not Frankl's. It was popularized by Stephen Covey, who said he found a version of it in a library book and thought it captured Frankl's teaching, but the line does not appear in Frankl's works. The Viktor Frankl Institute rejects the attribution; Quote Investigator points to a related 1963 passage by Rollo May. Frankl did teach that a person retains the freedom to choose an attitude, which is the idea Matt is after.

  • There is a proverb that says, in his heart a man plans his course, but the Lord determines his steps

    Verified

    Proverbs 16:9, here in the NIV: 'In their hearts humans plan their course, but the Lord establishes their steps.'

  • In the Book of Job, God puffs himself up at the end and says he made the Leviathan who could thwart his plans

    Incorrect

    In Job 41, God describes Leviathan as a creature no human can master, as a way of asking who then can stand against God. The 'thwart' language is Job's, not God's, and it runs the other way: in Job 42:2 Job says, 'I know that you can do all things; no purpose of yours can be thwarted.' Matt then folds in Romans 8:31, 'If God is for us, who can be against us?' and C.G. Jung's Answer to Job, which he was reading after his father died.

  • Sam Outlaw wrote a song whose lyrics are, 'Jesus, take the wheel and drive me to a bar. I'm going to do some drinking, so you should probably drive'

    Verified

    Sam Outlaw's 'Jesus Take the Wheel (And Drive Me to a Bar),' from the 2015 album Angeleno, includes 'Jesus take the wheel and drive me to a bar' and 'I'm gonna do some drinkin', so you should probably steer.' Matt blanks on the name and then lands it.
