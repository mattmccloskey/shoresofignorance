Fact Checks

  • Don Lemon entered a church service with protesters in Minnesota 'last weekend' and later called the congregants entitled by white supremacy

    Verified

    On January 18, 2026, Lemon livestreamed as anti-ICE protesters disrupted a service at Cities Church in St. Paul. In a January 19 interview with Jennifer Welch he said that entitlement 'comes from a supremacy, a white supremacy' and that his producer told him he became the face of it as 'a gay Black man in America.'

  • Minnesota is ground zero of the ICE controversy

    Verified

    As of January 2026, Minneapolis and St. Paul were the center of protests over a federal immigration enforcement surge, following the fatal ICE shooting of Renee Nicole Good earlier that month.

  • A founding father wrote that this is a Christian nation for Christians and it won't work any other way

    Needs Context

    Matt couldn't recall the source. The closest well-known line is John Adams's 1798 letter to the Massachusetts militia: 'Our Constitution was made only for a moral and religious People. It is wholly inadequate to the Government of any other.' Adams said moral and religious, not Christian specifically.

  • The Constitution itself is rife with very explicit Christian doctrine

    Incorrect

    The Constitution never mentions God or Christ. Its only religious references are the date formula 'in the Year of our Lord,' the Sundays-excepted clause for presidential vetoes, and Article VI's ban on religious tests for office. The explicit appeal to a Creator is in the Declaration of Independence, and the case for implicit Christian assumptions is a separate, arguable point.

  • The Declaration says all men are created equal with inalienable rights given by God

    Verified

    The Declaration (1776) says all men are 'endowed by their Creator with certain unalienable Rights.' It uses 'Creator' and 'Nature's God' rather than naming God directly in that line.

  • North Korean myth says the Kim leader doesn't defecate, and every home must display his portrait

    Needs Context

    Portraits of Kim Il Sung and Kim Jong Il are required in North Korean homes. The claim that state lore said Kim Jong Il never needed to defecate is widely repeated in Western media, but it is hard to trace to an official North Korean source.

  • Absolute power corrupts absolutely

    Verified

    Lord Acton, in an 1887 letter: 'Power tends to corrupt, and absolute power corrupts absolutely.'

  • Freud saw people as driven by animal instincts that must be restrained, while Rousseau saw people as naturally good and corrupted by society

    Verified

    This is a fair summary of Freud's id and civilization argument and Rousseau's account of natural goodness, and it matches how Carl Trueman frames both in The Rise and Triumph of the Modern Self (2020).

  • Philip Rieff describes three types of civilization, and a type 3 must mock the sacred order of a type 2

    Verified

    Rieff's late work, My Life Among the Deathworks (2006), describes first (pagan), second (faith) and third (no sacred order) worlds, and argues third-world culture lives by desecrating the second. Trueman popularized this framework. Rieff calls them worlds or cultures rather than types of civilization.

  • Peter Singer argues abortion should be legal up to about age 2

    Incorrect

    Singer argues newborns are not yet persons and that killing a severely disabled infant can be permissible. In Should the Baby Live? (1985) he and Helga Kuhse proposed a 28-day window, which he later called arbitrary. He has not proposed a limit of two years.

  • Singer justifies euthanizing elderly people perceived to not be experiencing pleasure in their life

    Needs Context

    Singer supports voluntary euthanasia and, in some cases, non-voluntary euthanasia for people who can no longer express a choice. He grounds this in preferences and suffering, not in a test of whether someone is experiencing pleasure.

  • Virginia just passed legalized full-term abortion, making nine states

    Needs Context

    On January 14 and 16, 2026, Virginia's legislature passed HJ1, a reproductive-freedom constitutional amendment. It is not law until voters approve it on November 3, 2026, and it still lets the state regulate third-trimester abortion. Roughly nine states plus DC set no gestational limit, so the count is close but Virginia was not yet one of them.

  • Most Americans are comfortable with abortion limits somewhere between six and 12 weeks

    Needs Context

    Gallup polling consistently finds about two-thirds of Americans support legal abortion in the first trimester (up to about 12 weeks) and far fewer in later trimesters. Support for a six-week limit specifically is much lower.

  • Nietzsche said we've killed God and there isn't enough water to wipe away the blood, and asked how we drank up the sea

    Verified

    The Gay Science, section 125 (1882): 'Who will wipe this blood off us? What water is there for us to clean ourselves?' and 'How could we drink up the sea?' Matt's paraphrase is close.

  • Chesterton's fence

    Verified

    From G.K. Chesterton's The Thing (1929): don't take down a fence until you know why it was put up.

  • The Soviet Union grew out of a society that was very Catholic for a very long time

    Incorrect

    Russia was overwhelmingly Eastern Orthodox, not Catholic. Catholicism was strong only in some later Soviet territories such as Lithuania and western Ukraine. The underlying point, that the USSR imposed atheism on a deeply religious society, holds.

  • Lenin and Stalin murdered tens of millions, and the Soviet Union collapsed at the end of the 1980s

    Needs Context

    Estimates of deaths from Soviet repression, famine and the Gulag under Lenin and Stalin run from several million to around 20 million depending on what is counted. The Berlin Wall fell in 1989, but the USSR formally dissolved in December 1991.

  • Berlin Wall guards were shooting in, not out

    Verified

    East German border guards had orders to stop escapes from the East. At least 140 people died at the Wall between 1961 and 1989.

  • Native Americans aren't exactly indigenous because they migrated here through the Bering Strait

    Needs Context

    Most researchers agree the ancestors of Native Americans arrived from Asia across Beringia at least 15,000 years ago. Indigenous means the first peoples of a place, though, and these were the first humans in the Americas, so the migration doesn't make them less indigenous.

  • The Aztecs sacrificed children to control the rain

    Verified

    Aztec priests sacrificed children to the rain god Tlaloc, and remains of dozens of children have been found at Tenochtitlan's Templo Mayor.

  • Planned Parenthood started with Margaret Sanger trying to breed out black people

    Needs Context

    Sanger was an open eugenicist who backed sterilization of the 'unfit,' and her 1939 Negro Project targeted Black communities with birth control. Historians dispute that her goal was to eliminate Black Americans, and she opposed abortion during her lifetime. Planned Parenthood disavowed her eugenics in 2020.

  • 60% of abortions in America are performed on Black women, who are about 6.5% of the population

    Incorrect

    CDC data for 2022 show non-Hispanic Black women accounted for 39.5% of abortions in the areas reporting race. Black Americans are about 13% of the U.S. population, so Black women are about 6.5 to 7%. The disparity is real, but the 60% figure is too high.

  • Euthanasia is the number one or two cause of death in Canada

    Incorrect

    Medically assisted death (MAID) accounted for 5.1% of Canadian deaths in 2024 (16,499 people). Health Canada does not rank it as a cause of death, and if it were counted separately it would rank about fifth, well behind cancer and heart disease.

  • C.S. Lewis's The Abolition of Man argues we can't get agreement on the good from science alone

    Verified

    Lewis (1943) argues that objective value, which he calls the Tao, can't be derived from scientific or naturalistic reasoning, and that rejecting it produces 'men without chests.'

  • Paul Rosolie was recently approached by a never-before-contacted tribe with six-foot bows and seven-foot arrows

    Needs Context

    Rosolie told Lex Fridman (episode #489, January 2026) about an encounter with the Mashco Piro on Peru's Las Piedras River. The encounter happened in October 2024 rather than a few months earlier, and the Mashco Piro are described as uncontacted or in voluntary isolation, not entirely unknown. The six-to-seven-foot arrows match his account.

  • The Sentinelese tribe lives on an island off the coast of India

    Verified

    North Sentinel Island is in India's Andaman Islands, and Indian law bars outsiders from approaching it.

  • The Emily Blunt Western about a British woman heading west is set in the late 1700s or early 1800s

    Incorrect

    The series is The English (2022), and it is set in 1890, near the end of the frontier era.

  • Sparta was a pagan civilization that deferred to the Oracle of Delphi

    Verified

    Spartans consulted the Pythia at Delphi on major decisions, and tradition held that Lycurgus's laws were sanctioned by the Oracle.

  • Annie Duke taught that the smarter you are, the more biased you are

    Verified

    In Thinking in Bets (2018), Duke cites research by West, Meserve and Stanovich (2012) finding that more cognitively sophisticated people are, if anything, more prone to the bias blind spot.

  • Veritasium showed that democracy is mathematically impossible

    Needs Context

    Veritasium's video 'Why Democracy Is Mathematically Impossible' explains Arrow's impossibility theorem: no ranked-choice voting system can satisfy every fairness criterion at once. That doesn't make democracy impossible, but it does mean every voting system involves trade-offs.

  • In The Brothers Karamazov, Ivan argues that if there is no God, everything is permitted

    Needs Context

    That is the idea Ivan voices in the novel (1880), though the exact phrase 'everything is permitted' is a popular summary rather than one quoted line.

  • Lethal injection is supposedly painless even though apparently it's not

    Needs Context

    There is real debate here. Autopsy studies of executed prisoners have found pulmonary edema consistent with suffering, and courts and states continue to argue over drug protocols.

  • H2S gas is heavier than air and can settle in low spots

    Verified

    Hydrogen sulfide is denser than air and can pool in low-lying areas, where high concentrations can kill within minutes.

  • Ali Larter, who plays the ex-wife in Landman, was the whipped cream scene in Varsity Blues

    Verified

    Larter plays Angela Norris in Landman and played Darcy in Varsity Blues (1999).
