Fact Checks

  • There was a law passed in the early 2000s that allowed the news to be biased, and Murdoch was the first person to jump on that and make Fox News a conservative news channel

    Incorrect

    The rule Matt is thinking of is the FCC's fairness doctrine, which required broadcast licensees to cover issues of public importance fairly. The FCC repealed it in 1987, not in the early 2000s, and it applied to broadcast licenses, not to cable. Fox News launched as a cable channel in 1996. Matt is right that something like a fairness rule ended, and that partisan cable news followed, but the date, the 'law,' and the causal link to Fox are all off.

  • Heather Heying wrote, as a plea to her fellow Americans, 'Do not take the bait. Do not feel it necessary to come to a conclusion. Do not even feel that it is your job to follow all of the news,' and that many who think they follow all the news follow only a slant, so are more misinformed than those who look away from the circus

    Verified

    That passage is from Heying's essay 'No Time For Words,' published in County Highway's March–April 2025 issue. Matt slightly loosens the wording in places ('this is my plea' for 'Here then is my plea,' 'a slant' for 'one slant'), but the attribution, the venue, and the argument are right. As of this recording, April 23, 2025, the piece was current.

  • Two different courts determined that Kilmar Abrego Garcia was MS-13, he was here illegally, he received a deportation order, and the only dispute is that he was not supposed to be deported to El Salvador

    Needs Context

    As of this recording in late April 2025, Abrego Garcia had been flown to El Salvador's CECOT prison on March 15 despite a final 2019 immigration order withholding his removal to that country. The government later called the removal an 'administrative error.' He had entered the United States illegally, and withholding of removal barred sending him to El Salvador but did not make him a citizen. The MS-13 claim was an administration allegation, denied by Abrego Garcia, and was not a clean finding of 'two different courts'; a 2019 immigration judge granted withholding because of threats from Barrio 18, and he had not been convicted of a crime. Michael's 'not to El Salvador' point tracks the withholding order; the gang determination and the 'pretty simple' framing do not.

  • Deporting the 20 million people who came here illegally is an 80/20 issue, and the majority of Democrats believe in this too

    Needs Context

    By early 2025, polling did show broad support for enforcing immigration law and for deporting people who had crossed the border illegally, and Trump-world often called that an 80/20 issue. '20 million' is a high-end political figure, not a settled demographic count (estimates of the unauthorized population and of recent encounters plus got-aways vary widely). Support among Democrats depends on the question: majorities often favored deportation of recent illegal crossers or of people with criminal records, not necessarily a single 20-million-person removal program.

  • MS-13 is considered a terrorist group

    Verified

    In February 2025 the State Department designated MS-13 a Foreign Terrorist Organization. Michael is using that designation, which was in force when they recorded.

  • The United States is paying El Salvador to lock up people it does not want, and Abrego Garcia is an El Salvadoran citizen imprisoned by El Salvador for crimes in El Salvador

    Needs Context

    The Trump administration had, by April 2025, a reported agreement with Nayib Bukele under which the United States paid El Salvador to hold deported people at CECOT. Abrego Garcia is a Salvadoran citizen who was being held there. 'Imprisoned for crimes in El Salvador' overstates what was known at the time: he had not been tried or convicted in a Salvadoran court for a crime that explained that confinement, and the U.S. government had already acknowledged that sending him there violated the 2019 withholding order.

  • The death of the Pope had just happened

    Verified

    Pope Francis died on April 21, 2025, two days before this episode was recorded on April 23. Matt is right that it had just happened.

  • John Vervaeke said that love is to will the good of the other for the other, and that insight has to come through a frame shift

    Verified

    'To love is to will the good of the other' is the classical formula, especially associated with Thomas Aquinas, and Vervaeke uses it in exactly this sense: the good is willed for the other, not for oneself. He also argues that insight depends on a change of framing. Matt is reporting Vervaeke's version of both ideas, not claiming to have coined them.

  • Thomas Jefferson, or maybe Benjamin Franklin in Poor Richard's Almanack, said that the problem with advice is that the fools won't take it and the wise don't need it

    Needs Context

    Neither the wording nor the Jefferson attribution holds. The closest Franklin line, in Poor Richard's Almanack, is the almost opposite thought: 'Fools need Advice most, but wise Men only are the better for it.' Matt's version is a floating aphorism, and looking up Poor Richard's Almanack mid-show is the right publication for a Franklin guess, but not for this sentence. What Franklin took to France during the Revolution was not the Almanack itself.
