Fact Checks

  • The official name is daylight saving time, not daylight savings time

    Verified

    Matt catches himself at the top: the standard name is daylight saving time, singular. In 2025, most of the United States sprang forward on Sunday, March 9, so his complaint about three hard mornings getting a 14-year-old up for school fits a Wednesday, March 12 recording.

  • Cynthia Lummis wants to put together a stablecoin bill before the bitcoin bill

    Needs Context

    On March 10, 2025, two days before they recorded, Lummis joined the reintroduced GENIUS Act, the Senate stablecoin bill (S. 919). On March 11 she introduced the BITCOIN Act (S. 954) to codify a strategic bitcoin reserve. The two bills were moving in the same week rather than one clearly being required to precede the other, but the stablecoin framework was the one already being reintroduced.

  • Donald Trump got into a Tesla Elon Musk brought to the White House and said, 'everything's computer,' and that line is going around as a meme on X

    Verified

    On March 11, 2025, the day before they recorded, Trump sat in a Tesla on the White House lawn with Musk and said, 'everything's computer.' The clip was already circulating.

  • Dostoevsky was exiled, then put in prison, then lined up on a firing line at about 26; at the last minute a stay of execution was ordered, but they fired on him anyway with blanks

    Incorrect

    On December 22, 1849, at Semyonovsky Square, the Petrashevsky prisoners had a death sentence read to them in a planned mock execution. Dostoevsky was 28, not 26, and was waiting in the second group of three. The first three men were tied to posts; the rifles were loaded with live ammunition, not blanks; a drumbeat of retreat came before any shots, and a messenger announced the Tsar's pardon. Dostoevsky had already spent months in the Peter and Paul Fortress. The exile to Siberian hard labor came after the mock execution, not before. He later wrote about facing death, including in The Idiot. Matt flags that he does not remember the reason, and is unsure about the epilepsy.

  • C.S. Lewis describes friendship as two people sitting side by side, facing the same direction, and Eros as two people facing each other

    Verified

    In The Four Loves, Lewis writes that lovers 'are normally face to face, absorbed in each other,' while friends 'stand side by side, their eyes looking ahead.' That is the distinction Matt uses for Wednesday nights versus a romantic pair.

  • G.K. Chesterton was an atheist, and in Orthodoxy he says he tried to find a heresy of his own, and when he had put the final touches on it he discovered that it was orthodoxy

    Needs Context

    The line is real. In Orthodoxy Chesterton writes, 'I did try to found a little heresy of my own; and when I had put the last touches to it, I discovered that it was orthodoxy.' Calling him simply an atheist who converted because he tried to be an atheist is too neat: he was raised Unitarian, passed through agnosticism, and the book is the record of that path, not a clean atheist-to-Christian jump.

  • Lewis says that marriage is a battle to the death, which no man of honor should deny it is

    Needs Context

    Matt is loose with the wording and already slightly hedging ('I think it's Lewis'). In The Four Loves Lewis does treat marriage under Eros as a total, till-death promise, and in Mere Christianity he writes about the 'fatal' seriousness of the vow, but the specific phrase 'a battle to the death, which no man of honor should deny' is not a well-attested Lewis line.

  • Aella is a sex worker and OnlyFans creator, is also an intellectual, has been on Triggernometry, and defends polyamory

    Verified

    Aella (the writer and analyst known for sex-work and survey work) appeared on Triggernometry and has publicly defended or discussed polyamory. Matt is careful to say he does not think she was the adult-film guest on 'Eric's podcast,' which is a different appearance.

  • Gad Saad says the number one predictor of child abuse is the presence of a stepparent in the home, and that it is 100 times more predictive than anything else on the usual list

    Needs Context

    Saad has said exactly this in interviews, including that a stepparent in the home is 'a hundred-fold more predictive' than the usual answers (alcohol, drugs, mental illness, a criminal history). The underlying finding is the Cinderella effect, from Martin Daly and Margo Wilson. Their strongest figure, on the order of 100 times, is for fatal batterings of very young children, not for all child abuse compared with every other predictor. A 1985 paper found roughly a 40-fold elevation in abuse cases for preschoolers living with a stepparent. The direction of the finding is real; the classroom version flattens it. Matt says he is taking Saad at his word and wants to look it up. The 'Katie something' he heard name it the Cinderella Problem the same day is Katy Faust, on Jordan Peterson's podcast.
