Fact Checks

  • Dostoevsky's Notes from Underground opens with a bitterly self-aware narrator who will not leave his underground posture

    Verified

    The 1864 novella begins with the Underground Man's diseased, spiteful self-description and his refusal of ordinary healthy life. Matt's synopsis is loose but the delicious-complaint reading is a standard interpretation.

  • In The Shawshank Redemption, Red (Morgan Freeman) fears parole and life on the outside after long institutionalization

    Verified

    Red's parole hearings and his fear of not belonging outside are central. Michael's key-unlocked-but-won't-leave image fits that theme.

  • Paul describes himself as a Pharisee of Pharisees and was converted after an encounter with Jesus on the road to Damascus

    Verified

    Philippians 3:5 and Acts 9. Michael's many-months-or-years formation period matches traditional readings that Paul spent substantial time after conversion before his public ministry.

  • People often stay in abusive relationships despite obvious harm

    Verified

    A well-documented pattern in domestic-violence research and clinical practice; Matt's point that familiarity and fear of starting over keep people stuck is standard.

  • Stockholm syndrome describes captives forming attachment to captors

    Verified

    The term arose from a 1973 Stockholm bank robbery hostage case and is used popularly for traumatic bonding. Matt links it to makeup-breakup attachment dynamics.

  • Band of Brothers depicts Easy Company through WWII including the Battle of the Bulge

    Verified

    The 2001 HBO miniseries follows Easy Company; episodes covering Bastogne dramatize the Bulge. Veterans' interviews appear in related documentary material.

  • After 9/11 many Americans described a temporary surge of national unity

    Verified

    Contemporary polling and cultural memory record elevated patriotism and cross-group solidarity in late 2001, followed by later polarization over Iraq, Afghanistan, and the Patriot Act — matching Matt and Michael's recollection.

  • Jewish shiva is a roughly week-long intensive mourning period with communal support and curtailed ordinary activity

    Needs Context

    Shiva traditionally lasts seven days, not two weeks; related stages (shloshim, year of mourning) extend further. Matt's forced stillness and meal-train parallel is directionally right; the two-week figure is approximate.

  • Isaac Asimov wrote Robots and Empire as part of his Robot series

    Verified

    Robots and Empire (1985) continues the Robot novels with human and robot viewpoints. Michael's last-of-the-robot-series memory is close enough for conversational citation.
