Fact Checks

  • The mass of a tree comes from the air, because the tree takes carbon dioxide, the sun breaks it apart, and the tree keeps the carbon and discards the oxygen

    Needs Context

    The headline is right and is the point of the Feynman clip: most of a tree's dry mass is carbon fixed from atmospheric CO2, not from soil, which is why a houseplant does not lower the soil line as it grows. Two details are soft. A living tree is also a lot of water taken up by the roots, and the oxygen released in photosynthesis comes mainly from water, not from the CO2 molecule the way Matt sketches it. Minerals from the soil are real but are only a small share of the mass.

  • Humans are made up of about 96 percent carbon

    Incorrect

    Carbon is only about 18 percent of body mass. The familiar 96 percent figure is for oxygen, carbon, hydrogen, and nitrogen taken together, most of that oxygen in water. Matt flags it as 'or something,' and the number is the usual one applied to the wrong element.

  • When you burn wood, the carbon recombines with oxygen as CO2, and the flame is the original solar energy being released

    Verified

    Combustion is photosynthesis run in reverse: stored chemical energy, fixed from sunlight, is released as heat and light while carbon returns to the air as carbon dioxide. That is the standard physical picture, and it is what Feynman is driving at.

  • James Nestor's book is called Breath

    Verified

    Breath: The New Science of a Lost Art (2019). Matt first says 'Breath or Breathe' and then correctly lands on Breath.

  • There is a disorder in which people orgasm constantly, and it is miserable

    Verified

    Persistent genital arousal disorder, sometimes discussed as persistent sexual arousal syndrome, is a rare condition in which unwanted arousal and, in some cases, spontaneous orgasms continue for long periods and can be highly distressing. Matt's 'too much of a good thing' reading matches how patients describe it.

  • Toddlers throw food off the high chair, and peekaboo, to learn object permanence

    Needs Context

    Object permanence is a real developmental milestone, and peekaboo is commonly used to play with it. Throwing food is messier: it can be about cause and effect, gravity, and getting a reaction, not only about checking whether the object still exists. The broader point about building mental models stands.

  • Love your neighbor as yourself is a foundational biblical command

    Verified

    Leviticus 19:18, and Jesus names it alongside love of God as the heart of the law in Matthew 22:39 and Mark 12:31. That is the axiom Michael says he had drifted from.

  • Jordan Peterson teaches that at a perceptual level we see tools and obstacles, not objects

    Verified

    This is a standard Peterson claim, drawn from affordance theory and Heidegger, and 'when all you have is a hammer, everything looks like a nail' is the older Maslow line Matt folds into it. The grocery-aisle and table-in-the-hallway examples are fair illustrations.

  • The top 30 percent of men get 80 percent of the women, a Pareto distribution, which is the kind of thing 'ho math' talks about

    Needs Context

    Dating-app data often show a skewed distribution of messages and ratings, and Pareto's 80/20 rule is real as a statistical pattern. The jump from that to '30 percent of men get 80 percent of the women' in actual pairing is a popular simplification from the manosphere account Michael is remembering, not a clean demographic fact.

  • 1883 portrays the Oregon Trail, with Polish immigrants hauling pianos and armoires

    Needs Context

    1883 is a Taylor Sheridan series about a wagon train leaving Texas toward the northern Rockies and Oregon country. It does show European immigrant families reluctant to abandon heavy heirlooms. It is not a straight retelling of the classic Independence-to-Oregon Trail, and 'Polish immigrants' is looser than the show's mix of characters.

  • In June or July 2025, Eric Weinstein went on a podcast and told a childless guest he needed to have kids, or he would not see the future

    Verified

    Weinstein appeared on Steven Bartlett's Diary of a CEO on July 14, 2025, and pressed the childless host on marrying and having children as the way to care about the future. Michael has the month and the thrust right.

  • Michael Saylor has no kids and is very invested in the future, while Elon Musk is also invested in the future and is very into having kids

    Verified

    Public biographies treat Saylor as unmarried and without children. Musk has many children and has repeatedly argued that having kids is a civilizational duty. Matt is using them as contrasting examples, not as a full psychological study.

  • There have been 50 million years of uninterrupted sexual procreation

    Needs Context

    Sexual reproduction in eukaryotes is on the order of a billion years old, and even the primate story is longer and messier than a clean 50-million-year figure. The moral — the line continued because people lived as if there would be children — does not depend on that number.

  • Irvin Yalom said that in the final analysis the only thing one can truly want is God

    Incorrect

    The line is how Matt puts a claim about aim, but it is a bad fit for Yalom, a secular existential psychiatrist who treats the wish for God as a wish to escape death rather than as the one true object of desire. Matt presents it tentatively ('I feel like it's Irvin Yalam'), and the attribution does not hold.

  • In The Origins and History of Consciousness, Erich Neumann says the feminine is what is and the masculine is what becomes, and he or Jung may have coined the ouroboros

    Needs Context

    Neumann does treat the feminine as the containing, uroboric ground of being and the masculine as the ego that emerges and differentiates, so Matt's 'what is / what becomes' is a fair popularization. The ouroboros itself is an ancient Egyptian and Greek symbol; Jung and Neumann used it, they did not invent it. Matt is asking, not asserting, who coined it.

  • Cafe Medici started in 2006, almost 19 years before this recording

    Verified

    Michael and Alison Vaclav opened Caffé Medici's West Lynn shop in September 2006. This episode was recorded on the night of August 20, 2025, so 'almost 19 years' is right.
