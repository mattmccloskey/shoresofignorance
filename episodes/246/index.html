Fact Checks

  • Jordan Peterson says to tell the truth, and that whatever occurs on account of telling the truth is the best possible thing that could happen

    Verified

    This is a standard Peterson formulation, close to his 'tell the truth, or at least don't lie,' and to the claim that the consequence of honesty is preferable to the consequence of a lie, even when it hurts. Matt is paraphrasing, not quoting a single canonical sentence.

  • Ben Affleck told Theo Von that paparazzi get the worst possible shots of him because they catch him when he is out and does not want to be out

    Verified

    Affleck said as much on This Past Weekend episode 578, which came out April 24, 2025. He talked about being photographed when he is with his kids or trying to get somewhere, looking irritated, and about photographers pushing for a reaction.

  • In the Gospel of John, Jesus tells the disciples several times that if they ask for anything in his name it will be given, without qualifications

    Needs Context

    John 14:13-14, 15:16, and 16:23-24 do repeat the promise, which is why it stuck with Matt. The surrounding chapters also talk about remaining in him and asking according to that relationship, so 'no qualifications' is stronger than a full reading of John, even if the bare sentences sound unqualified.

  • Texas, including Austin ISD, is banning phones in schools

    Verified

    Texas HB 1481, effective for the 2025-26 school year, requires public schools to keep student personal devices off and put away during the day. Austin ISD posted its announcement on August 13, 2025, the same day this episode was recorded, and Matt says he had just learned it.

  • Studies of voluntary school phone bans found that every kid afterward said it was better

    Needs Context

    There is a growing set of school-ban studies and student surveys that often find less distraction and, after the fact, more support than students expected. 'Every one of the kids' is stronger than those results, and Michael already flags the stories as maybe anecdotal.

  • 90 percent of people in the United States live better than kings and queens did 100 years ago

    Needs Context

    Modern Americans have medicines, appliances, and information that no 1925 monarch had, and writers in the Rosling and Pinker tradition make versions of this claim. '100 years ago' is a softer contrast than medieval or early-modern kings, who already had palaces, staff, travel, and, by the 1920s, electricity, cars, and telephones. The direction is fair; the exact 90 percent and the century mark are rhetorical.

  • Rates of diabetes and obesity in the United States are at epidemic levels

    Verified

    As of this recording, U.S. adult obesity remained near 37 percent, down slightly from a 2022 peak but still historically very high, and diabetes, diagnosed and undiagnosed, was around 14 to 16 percent of adults. Calling both epidemic is ordinary public-health language, not a stretch.

  • The food pyramid was a lie made up by the tobacco industry in the 1980s

    Incorrect

    The USDA Food Guide Pyramid was released in 1992, not in the 1980s. Tobacco companies did own major food brands in that era and used some of the same product-design habits on processed food, which is likely the story Matt is remembering. They did not invent the pyramid, and the fight over the pyramid is more often told as a meat-and-dairy lobbying story.

  • Official advice that eggs, butter, and cholesterol are bad for you has since been walked back

    Verified

    U.S. dietary guidance long treated dietary cholesterol and animal fats as villains. The 2015 Dietary Guidelines dropped cholesterol as a nutrient of concern, and later advice has been more relaxed about eggs and, in some camps, butter. Matt is right that the old rule was too simple, and also right to wonder whether the new one will move again.

  • Bruce Springsteen gave a SXSW keynote in 2009 or 2010 in which he said the right camera is the camera you have and the right guitar is the guitar you have

    Needs Context

    The keynote was March 15, 2012, in Austin, so the year is off, and the wording Matt has carried for years is a blend. What Springsteen did say is that 'there is no right way, no pure way, of doing it,' only doing it, which is the same spirit as the photography adage 'the best camera is the one that's with you.'

  • There are five genres and not more than five, and you have to write in a known genre or the audience will stop watching

    Needs Context

    Genre systems abound — comedy, drama, horror, romance, and so on, or Blake Snyder's ten, or Booker's seven plots — but there is no standard film-school rule that there are exactly five. The underlying advice, that audiences need a recognizable frame before you can subvert it, is ordinary screenwriting wisdom.

  • America was not, at a glance, founded on slavery, and its founding principle is individual sovereignty and inalienable rights given by God

    Needs Context

    The Declaration does ground rights in a Creator and in human equality, and the Constitution is a charter of limited government. It also contained the three-fifths clause and a fugitive-slave provision, and slavery existed in the colonies at the founding. Matt is arguing against the slogan that the country was founded on slavery, not claiming slavery was absent, and the tension is the whole historical argument.

  • Lincoln held off the end of the Civil War so he could get the 13th Amendment passed, as shown in Spielberg's Lincoln

    Needs Context

    The House passed the 13th Amendment on January 31, 1865, and Lincoln met Confederate commissioners at Hampton Roads on February 3. He did want abolition locked into the Constitution rather than left as a wartime measure, and the film makes that the spine of the plot. Historians generally say the movie tightens and dramatizes a delay that was more about terms of peace than a simple scheme to hold the war open until the vote.

  • Daniel Day-Lewis played Bill the Butcher in Gangs of New York, stays in character while filming, and often disappears for years between movies

    Verified

    He played William 'Bill the Butcher' Cutting, and he is famous for immersive preparation and for long gaps between roles. Matt later rewatched There Will Be Blood, which is also his. Tommy Lee Jones plays Thaddeus Stevens in Lincoln, as Matt says.

  • Method acting in some sense killed Philip Seymour Hoffman, Heath Ledger, and, in a different manner, Robin Williams

    Needs Context

    Hoffman died of a 2014 overdose, Ledger of an accidental overdose in 2008 after The Dark Knight, and Williams died by suicide in 2014 with Lewy body dementia a major part of the story. The popular idea that a role, or method acting as such, killed them outruns what is known, and Matt already softens it to 'in some sense' and 'in a different manner.'

  • Elon Musk runs five really successful companies, is not in a relationship, and at least one of his kids has disowned him

    Needs Context

    By August 2025 Musk was associated with more than five companies, including Tesla, SpaceX, xAI, X, Neuralink, and the Boring Company, and his relationship status was a moving target. The disowning is right: Vivian Jenna Wilson publicly broke with him. Michael is reaching for the cost-of-greatness point, not doing a precise headcount.

  • Before the last 75 years, no one was a successful artist in their lifetime; Leonardo, Monet, Picasso, and Van Gogh were just struggling dudes whose work became famous later

    Incorrect

    Van Gogh is the classic case of posthumous fame. Leonardo was a celebrated court artist, Monet became wealthy from his work, and Picasso was famous and rich in his own lifetime. The broader point that many artists die before the culture catches up does not hold for that list as a group.

  • The Mona Lisa was stolen from a museum in the 1970s, recovered some years later, and only then became famous

    Incorrect

    Vincenzo Peruggia stole it from the Louvre in 1911, not in the 1970s, and it was recovered in late 1913, about 28 months later. The theft and the newspaper hunt did turn an already important painting into a worldwide celebrity, so the fame thesis is fair and the decade is not.

  • Joe Rogan has said he made about $50,000 a week on a sitcom and hated it

    Verified

    Rogan has told this story about NewsRadio, including on his podcast with Josh Brolin, which is likely the clip Michael had been watching. He has used the $50,000-a-week figure and said the work felt like a trap even though the money was great. Michael already allows that it might have been a week or a month.

  • God told Abraham that if he went out on the adventure he would become a blessing to himself and to those around him

    Verified

    Genesis 12:1-3 is the call: go from your country, and 'I will bless you' and 'in you all the families of the earth shall be blessed.' Matt's wording is looser than the text, but that is the promise he is remembering.
