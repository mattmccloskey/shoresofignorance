Fact Checks

  • The you put your weed in it skit was on Saturday Night Live in the 80s or maybe the 90s

    Verified

    It aired in 1993, with Rob Schneider as the clerk of an Out of Africa primitive art shop who tells every customer that each artifact is where you put your weed. Schneider has said a visit to an African antiques shop in Rochester, New York inspired it.

  • A grain of fentanyl the size of a grain of salt will kill you

    Needs Context

    As of November 2025 the DEA said 2 milligrams of fentanyl can be a lethal dose, and its first-responder guide compares 2 to 3 milligrams to about five to seven grains of table salt, so a single grain overstates it. The risk depends on body size, tolerance and past use, and the DEA's broader warning about laced pills stands, since it has reported that a large share of the counterfeit pills it tests contain a potentially lethal dose.

  • Michael asked who thought making fentanyl was a good idea

    Verified

    The Belgian chemist Paul Janssen first made fentanyl in 1959 and 1960 as a powerful painkiller, and it was introduced in the 1960s as an intravenous anesthetic for surgery, which is still a major legitimate use.

  • Chase Hughes is ex-CIA, or rather Navy intelligence, and wrote many of the books on interrogation for operatives and spies

    Needs Context

    Hughes is not ex-CIA. He served about 20 years in the US Navy, rising to chief petty officer, in jobs that included signalman, navigator and detainee operations, and he trained in interviewing and interrogation along the way. His books, including The Ellipsis Manual, Six-Minute X-Ray and The Behavior Operations Manual, are marketed to intelligence and law enforcement audiences, but he was not formally a naval intelligence officer.

  • Studies show that the name you choose for your child shapes their life and how people treat them

    Needs Context

    There is real evidence that names affect how others respond, such as the 2004 Bertrand and Mullainathan study in which résumés with stereotypically white names got more callbacks than identical ones with stereotypically Black names. Stronger claims that names steer personality or career, sometimes called nominative determinism, have mostly failed to replicate or are explained by other factors.

  • About 10 years ago corporations were buying unconscious bias training

    Verified

    Unconscious or implicit bias training spread widely through large companies in the mid-2010s, with Google, Facebook and later Starbucks among the best-known examples. A 2019 meta-analysis found such interventions changed scores on implicit measures only slightly and did not reliably change behavior.

  • Dog and Duck was a place where everybody knew them, and then they took it from us

    Verified

    The Dog & Duck Pub closed its longtime West 17th Street location in central Austin on October 31, 2014, when the building was set to be redeveloped. It reopened on Webberville Road in East Austin and closed there in late 2017.

  • In McGilchrist's account, learning a piece on piano starts in the right hemisphere, moves to the left as it becomes mechanical, and moves back to the right when you really play it

    Needs Context

    This is a fair summary of the pattern McGilchrist describes in The Master and His Emissary, where something new is first grasped by the right hemisphere, worked out and made routine by the left, and then returned to the right as a lived whole. Neuroscientists agree that music draws on both hemispheres, but the neat right, left, right sequence is McGilchrist's interpretive framework rather than settled science.

  • The composer Philip Glass wrote a piece for orchestra called Tacit in which every part is a rest

    Incorrect

    The piece is John Cage's 4′33″, composed in 1952 and first performed by the pianist David Tudor in Woodstock, New York. It can be played by any instrument or ensemble, including orchestras, and each of its three movements is marked tacet, Latin for it is silent, not without. Cage's intent was the one Matt described, to make the audience notice the ambient sounds of the room. The show's description for this episode already notes the mix-up.

  • The concept of deathworks was developed not by the author of The Rise and Triumph of the Modern Self but by another author

    Verified

    Carl Trueman, author of The Rise and Triumph of the Modern Self (2020), takes the term from the sociologist Philip Rieff, whose posthumously published book My Life among the Deathworks (2006) describes works that use the sacred symbols of an earlier culture in order to subvert and destroy them.

  • Piss Christ is a small Jesus on a crucifix submerged in a container of the artist's urine, and it is given as an example of a deathwork

    Verified

    Andres Serrano's 1987 work is a photograph of a small plastic crucifix submerged in a glass of his own urine. Rieff discusses it in My Life among the Deathworks, and Trueman calls it a paradigm example of what Rieff meant.

  • The most recent Olympics in Paris included a deathwork mocking Christianity

    Needs Context

    A tableau at the July 26, 2024 opening ceremony with drag performers around a long table was widely read as a parody of Leonardo's Last Supper and drew protests from Catholic bishops and others. Artistic director Thomas Jolly said it was not inspired by the Last Supper but was a pagan feast tied to Dionysus, art historians pointed to Jan van Bijlert's The Feast of the Gods, and Paris 2024 apologized to anyone who was offended.

  • The rapper who had the country song made a music video ending with him performing a sex act on Satan

    Needs Context

    The artist is Lil Nas X, whose country rap song Old Town Road was a record-breaking hit in 2019. In the 2021 video for Montero (Call Me by Your Name), he slides down a pole into hell and gives Satan a lap dance before snapping his neck and taking his horns. The video is sexually suggestive, but it does not show the act Matt described.

  • A banana duct-taped to a wall was a real artwork that sold for about $10 million

    Needs Context

    The work is Maurizio Cattelan's Comedian, first shown at Art Basel Miami Beach in 2019, when editions sold for $120,000 to $150,000. On November 20, 2024, an edition sold at Sotheby's New York for $6.2 million including fees to the crypto entrepreneur Justin Sun, who later ate the banana. That is real millions, though well short of $10 million.

  • Banksy remotely shredded a piece as soon as it was sold, and the shredder jammed so the top quarter is still in the frame

    Needs Context

    On October 5, 2018, Banksy's Girl with Balloon passed through a shredder hidden in its frame seconds after selling at Sotheby's London for £1,042,000. The shredder stopped about halfway, not three quarters of the way, leaving the top half intact. Banksy later said it was meant to shred completely and had worked every time in rehearsal. Renamed Love Is in the Bin, it resold at Sotheby's in October 2021 for £18,582,000.

  • Everybody says Pet Sounds is the rival to Abbey Road for the best album of all time

    Needs Context

    Pet Sounds is a perennial contender. It topped Mojo's 1995 list and ranked second on Rolling Stone's 500 greatest albums in 2003 and 2020. Its usual rival at the top is another Beatles album, Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band, which Rolling Stone ranked first in 2003. Abbey Road placed 14th that year and fifth in 2020.

  • Brian Wilson, the lead singer of the Beach Boys, struggled with mental health and was maybe diagnosed with bipolar schizophrenia

    Needs Context

    Wilson was the band's main songwriter and producer and sang lead on many songs, though Mike Love and others sang lead on many of the hits. He was diagnosed with schizoaffective disorder, which combines psychotic symptoms with mood episodes, and he spoke for years about hearing voices. He died on June 11, 2025, at 82, a few months before this episode was recorded.

  • In The Lord of the Rings, Gandalf tells Frodo, Many that live deserve death and some that die deserve life. Can you give it to them?

    Verified

    Matt's final version is nearly word for word the passage from The Shadow of the Past in The Fellowship of the Ring, which goes on, Then do not be too eager to deal out death in judgement. For even the very wise cannot see all ends. I have not much hope that Gollum can be cured before he dies, but there is a chance of it. In The Two Towers, Frodo recalls the conversation when he and Sam capture Gollum.

  • Gollum saved Sméagol, and Sméagol later rids himself of Gollum and is free again

    Needs Context

    The scene Michael described is from Peter Jackson's film The Two Towers (2002), where Gollum claims that we survived because of me and Sméagol then orders him to leave. It is invented for the film, and the freedom is short-lived, since Gollum returns after Sméagol feels betrayed at the Forbidden Pool. In Tolkien's book, the two voices argue but Sméagol is never rid of Gollum.

  • Working out your salvation with fear and trembling

    Verified

    The phrase is from Philippians 2:12, where Paul writes, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling, for it is God who works in you.

  • God is dead is not a celebration but a mournful statement, and Nietzsche tried to answer it with the Übermensch

    Verified

    In The Gay Science (1882), the madman who announces God's death asks, How shall we comfort ourselves, the murderers of all murderers? Nietzsche presents it as a crisis rather than a victory. The Übermensch, introduced in Thus Spoke Zarathustra, is his picture of the human being who creates values after that loss.

  • Bret Weinstein calls religion metaphorically true, while Jordan Peterson says he acts as if God is real

    Verified

    Weinstein has long described religious beliefs as literally false but metaphorically true, meaning they are adaptive even if not factual. Peterson has repeatedly said that he acts as if God exists and lets others judge whether he believes.

  • C.S. Lewis makes the point in The Four Loves that if you can't get men to fight for their nation, you have to get them to fight for something like humanity

    Verified

    In the chapter on love of country, Lewis writes that if people will spend neither sweat nor blood for their country, they must be made to feel that they are spending them for justice, or civilisation, or humanity, and that this is a step down, not up.

  • The words one nation under God in the Pledge of Allegiance

    Needs Context

    Under God was not in the original 1892 pledge. Congress added it in 1954, during the Cold War, so it has been part of the pledge for about 70 years rather than from the start.
