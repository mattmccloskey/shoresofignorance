About This Episode

Matt has been feeling like the good days are behind him, nostalgic for the 90s and for a team that broke up after COVID. Michael is more caught on what's next, with a daughter heading to college and no new shop to open.

A line from Streams in the Desert stays with us: the past is forgiven, the present is full of power, and the future is full of hope. We get into why God doesn't show us what's coming, marriage vows as a standard to correct toward, and knowing the truth the way you know a person.

Fact Checks

  • Streams in the Desert line on the past, present, and future

    Verified

    Matt (12:10) quoted, "the past is forgiven, the present is full of power, and the future is full of hope," from his morning copy of Streams in the Desert, and could not remember the date. It is the June 26 entry, "Unbelief," citing the painter and writer James Smetham: "all the past is forgiven, and all the present furnished with power, and all the future bright with hope." Matt's wording is a close paraphrase ("full of" for "furnished with" and "bright with").

  • Cutting off a note's attack hides the instrument

    Needs Context

    Matt (15:20) said that if you record piano, violin, guitar, and clarinet on the same note and cut off the attack, "you will have a very difficult time" telling them apart. The finding is real and dates at least to work by Jean-Claude Risset and Max Mathews, and to Charles A. Elliott's 1975 study in the Journal of Research in Music Education: with attacks and releases removed, graduate music students identified only a few instruments (clarinet, oboe, trumpet) at significant rates. Identification gets much harder, but it does not collapse for every instrument, and Matt's four-instrument set would not all be equally confusing.

  • The Logos holding all things is in "two Colossians"

    Needs Context

    Matt (20:08) pointed back to their Logos episode and said "I think it's in two Colossians, Paul talks about the Logos that was with God at the beginning, brought reality into being and holds reality in being now." The holding-together claim is Colossians 1:16-17: "all things were created through him and for him... and in him all things hold together." There is no "2 Colossians." The word Logos, and "in the beginning," is from John 1:1-3.

  • The mass of a tree comes from the air

    Verified

    Matt (23:10) repeated a point they spent Ep 247 on. A tree's dry mass is mostly carbon taken from carbon dioxide in the air through photosynthesis, not from the soil.

  • Biff got rich with a baseball playbook

    Needs Context

    Matt (29:27) said Biff "gets very rich by bringing the play, the baseball playbook, back from the future." In Back to the Future Part II it is Grays Sports Almanac, covering many sports, not a baseball playbook.

  • Churchill said plans are of little importance, but planning is essential

    Incorrect

    Matt (35:49) looked the line up and attributed it to Churchill. Wikiquote lists that wording as misattributed. The closest famous version is Eisenhower in 1957: "Plans are worthless, but planning is everything." Similar military aphorisms have also been tied to Moltke and Clausewitz.

  • The "four rules of planning" are from The Flash

    Verified

    Matt (35:49) quoted, "make the plan, execute the plan, expect the plan to go off the rails, throw away the plan." That is Leonard Snart (Captain Cold) in The Flash, season 3, episode 22, "Infantino Street" (2017): "There are only four rules you need to remember: make the plan, execute the plan, expect the plan to go off the rails, throw away the plan."

  • Tim Cook resigned and Jonathan Turns is Apple's CEO

    Needs Context

    Matt (40:49) said Cook "resigned," that "Jonathan Turns is CEO now," and that Apple had just put out a foldable iPhone Duo and a touchscreen MacBook Pro. John Ternus did become CEO on September 1, 2026, and both products are real (the Duo was announced in September, the touchscreen MacBook Pro in late October). Cook did not resign. He became executive chairman, announced April 20, 2026. The name is John Ternus, not Jonathan Turns.

  • Steve Jobs said people don't want to poke a vertical screen in the iPhone keynote

    Needs Context

    Matt (41:09) mixed the 2007 iPhone announcement (one home button, no keypad) with a later Jobs argument that touching a vertical computer screen is a bad experience because your arm gets tired. He even started to correct himself toward the iPad keynote. The "ergonomically terrible" comments, often called gorilla arm, are from October 2010, around "Back to the Mac," about touchscreen Macs, not the original iPhone unveil.

  • Iran is a major funder of Hamas and Hezbollah

    Verified

    Michael (48:26) said Iran "basically funds all the Hamas and [Hezbollah] and those other groups" and is "a large funder of those things." U.S. assessments have long described Iran as a principal financial and military backer of both groups, on the order of about $100 million a year to Hamas and several hundred million a year to Hezbollah, plus weapons and training.

  • "Wives, submit" is in Corinthians

    Incorrect

    Matt (67:38) called it "the big controversial verse in Corinthians." The passage is Ephesians 5:22-25. His follow-up is right: the next charge to husbands is to love their wives "as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her."

  • In heaven the lamb lays down with the lion

    Needs Context

    Michael (54:18) used the familiar "lamb lays down with the lion" picture of heaven. Isaiah 11:6 says "the wolf shall dwell with the lamb," with the leopard, the calf, and the lion in the same verse; Isaiah 65:25 has the wolf and the lamb, and the lion eating straw. The lion-and-lamb pairing is a long-standing popular paraphrase, not the exact wording.

  • The legal definition of marriage is consummation

    Needs Context

    Matt (71:51) hedged ("I don't know if it's still this way") but said "generally we think of the legal definition of marriage" as consummation, not the ceremony or certificate, and that you could get an annulment if the marriage was not consummated. In U.S. civil law, a licensed ceremony is what makes a marriage valid. Some states still allow annulment for inability or refusal to consummate, and in Catholic canon law a valid but unconsummated marriage can be dissolved by the pope, which is a different process from a declaration of nullity. Consummation matters in some traditions; it is not the ordinary legal definition.

  • Royal consummation was public

    Needs Context

    Matt (72:04) said that "when royalty got married, the consummation was public," and Michael agreed ("The watch. Yeah."). Medieval and early modern courts did have bedding ceremonies, in which attendants escorted the couple to bed and a blessing was given so the union could be witnessed as real. Guests usually then withdrew. It was a public assurance, not typically public intercourse, and the custom had faded by the end of the 17th century.

  • Peterson says once you have kids, there's no such thing as divorce

    Needs Context

    Matt (73:05) said, "I think Peterson says, once you have kids, there's no such thing as divorce," and immediately added that he thought it was hyperbolic. Peterson has argued that divorce should be much harder to obtain when children are involved, and that parents remain bound through their kids. The exact wording was not located; the spirit of the claim is in his marriage talks, not a literal legal statement.

  • Solomon had 500 wives and 700 concubines

    Incorrect

    Matt (76:12) said he had looked it up and thought Solomon had "like, 500" wives, "plus 700 concubines," plus "another category." 1 Kings 11:3 says "He had 700 wives, who were princesses, and 300 concubines." The numbers are reversed and inflated, and the text does not add a third category.

  • God said Solomon didn't ask for women

    Needs Context

    Matt (75:20) recalled God saying Solomon "didn't ask for riches or glory or women or power." In 1 Kings 3:11-13, God says Solomon did not ask for long life, riches, or the life of his enemies, and then also gives him riches and honor. Women are not in that speech. Solomon's many wives come later, in 1 Kings 11.

  • The people having the most sex are married Christians

    Needs Context

    Matt (78:00) said he "saw this statistic somewhat recently that the people having the most sex are monogamous, married Christians." The closest recent study is the Institute for Family Studies' September 2026 report "The Devout Difference," using 2023-2024 General Social Survey data on married Americans ages 20 to 54: 62% of regular churchgoers had sex at least weekly, compared with 48% of nonreligious married couples. That is more frequent sex among married churchgoers than among nonreligious married peers. It is not a finding that this group has the most sex of anyone, including unmarried people.
