Fact Checks

  • Beards evolved to protect against punches, and studies show fewer lacerations

    Needs Context

    A 2020 University of Utah study (Beseris et al.) found that fur-covered models absorbed more blunt-impact energy than bare ones. It used sheepskin on bone-like composites, not human faces, and didn't measure lacerations.

  • Chase Hughes has a new book about language as a parasite

    Verified

    Tongue: A Cognitive Hazard (Evergreen Press, published November 19, 2025). Hughes describes language as acting 'more like a parasite than a friend.'

  • 'Name it to tame it' helps with emotional regulation

    Verified

    Coined by psychiatrist Dan Siegel and supported by affect-labeling research (Lieberman et al., 2007), which shows that naming emotions dampens amygdala activity.

  • C. S. Lewis's Four Loves says each friend brings out a different part of a person

    Verified

    Lewis's own example: after Charles Williams died, he lost the part of Tolkien that only Charles could bring out. Matt's 'Karen' illustration is his own paraphrase of the idea.

  • Your optic nerve maps straight onto your brain stem, so you react before you're conscious

    Needs Context

    Most visual signals go to the visual cortex, but a subcortical route (superior colliculus to pulvinar to amygdala) supports fast, pre-conscious threat responses.

  • Your gut has more neurons than your entire brain

    Incorrect

    The enteric nervous system has roughly 200–600 million neurons, compared with about 86 billion in the brain. It has more than the spinal cord, not the brain.

  • Cats really do freak out at cucumbers

    Verified

    The viral videos are real. Experts attribute the startle to an unexpected object appearing silently behind the cat, possibly resembling a snake.

  • Viktor Frankl: 'Between stimulus and response there is a space…'

    Needs Context

    Popularized by Stephen Covey and widely credited to Frankl, but it doesn't appear in Frankl's published works. Its true origin is unclear.

  • Writing Down the Bones teaches first thoughts and keeping your hand moving

    Verified

    Natalie Goldberg (1986): timed writing, keep your hand moving, and 'first thoughts' before the internal editor kicks in.

  • Fear increases pain in childbirth

    Verified

    Research on fear of childbirth links higher fear to greater perceived labor pain, the 'fear-tension-pain' cycle Grantly Dick-Read described in the 1930s.

  • 'Do not fear' is the most repeated command in the Bible

    Needs Context

    A popular claim, sometimes put at '365 times'. Counts of 'fear not' and 'do not be afraid' run to roughly 100 or more, which makes it one of the most frequent commands, but rankings vary by method.

  • The fear of God is the beginning of knowledge

    Verified

    Proverbs 1:7; also Proverbs 9:10 ('beginning of wisdom').

  • The Bible uses the same word for 'do not fear' and 'fear the Lord'

    Verified

    Alison wondered about this. In Hebrew, both commonly use the root yare' (ירא), and in Greek both use phobeō.

  • Carl Jung: all neuroses are substitutes for legitimate suffering

    Verified

    Jung, Psychology and Religion (1938; Collected Works Vol. 11, §129): 'neurosis is always a substitute for legitimate suffering.' Matt's dad evidently quoted it too.

  • Solzhenitsyn said everything that happens is your responsibility

    Needs Context

    The idea that 'each of us is responsible for everyone and everything' is most famously Dostoevsky's, through Father Zosima in The Brothers Karamazov. Solzhenitsyn wrote of the line between good and evil running through every heart.

  • The Black Gates of Mordor were originally built by Gondor

    Verified

    The Morannon was built by Gondor to guard Mordor after Sauron's defeat. It was later abandoned, and Sauron took it over.

  • God tells Job 'who can thwart the plans of the Almighty'

    Needs Context

    In Job 42:2 it's Job who says to God, 'no purpose of yours can be thwarted.' A similar line from God appears in Isaiah 14:27.

  • Jesus delayed two days when Lazarus died

    Verified

    John 11:6.

  • Jesus slept in the boat during a storm

    Verified

    Mark 4:38; also Matthew 8:24 and Luke 8:23.
