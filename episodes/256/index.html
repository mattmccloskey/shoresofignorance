Fact Checks

  • Love the person, hate the sinner is not a Bible verse

    Verified

    Michael's slip aside, the usual saying is love the sinner, hate the sin, and it is not in the Bible. It is usually traced to Augustine's Letter 211, written around 424, which calls for due love for the persons and hatred of the sin, and Gandhi used a version in his 1929 autobiography.

  • Jesus says to look at the lilies of the field and the birds, which God clothes and feeds

    Verified

    Matthew 6:26-30 says your heavenly Father feeds the birds of the air and clothes the grass of the field, and asks, will he not much more clothe you, O you of little faith?

  • There is a saying to be innocent as lambs and wise as serpents

    Incorrect

    In Matthew 10:16 Jesus tells the disciples to be wise as serpents and innocent as doves, not lambs. Michael was unsure and called it more of a saying than a parable, which it is.

  • Jesus tells his disciples to be like the children who come to him

    Verified

    In Matthew 18:3 Jesus says that unless you turn and become like children, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven. In Matthew 19:14 he says, Let the little children come to me.

  • In The Chosen, before Jesus picks his disciples, children discover him camping out and come every day to ask questions like what his favorite food is

    Verified

    This is season 1, episode 3, Jesus Loves the Little Children, released in 2019. Jesus is alone at his camp in the countryside and a girl named Abigail and her friends visit him repeatedly and ask him childlike questions, before he calls Simon and Andrew in the next episode.

  • Jesus says that if you ask anything in his name it will be given, and that the disciples haven't asked him for anything

    Verified

    John 14:13-14 says Whatever you ask in my name, this I will do, and in John 16:24 Jesus says, Until now you have asked nothing in my name. Ask, and you will receive, that your joy may be full. Matt framed the frustration as his own reading of it.

  • You can't look at the face of God without dying

    Verified

    In Exodus 33:20 God tells Moses, You cannot see my face, for man shall not see me and live.

  • Matt was raised to love his wife the way Christ loves the church

    Verified

    The teaching is Ephesians 5:25: Husbands, love your wives, as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her.

  • Paul writes in Romans, maybe chapter 8 or 7, that what he wants to do he does not do, and what he hates he does

    Verified

    It is Romans 7:15: For I do not do what I want, but I do the very thing I hate, repeated in 7:19.

  • TDCJ is the Department of Criminal Justice

    Verified

    TDCJ is the Texas Department of Criminal Justice, which runs one of the largest prison systems in the United States.

  • Elon Musk, or some lawyer, said that with the laws currently on the books every single American is guilty of a crime

    Needs Context

    The best-known version of the claim is the civil liberties lawyer Harvey Silverglate's 2009 book Three Felonies a Day, which argues that vague federal laws mean the average professional unknowingly commits about three felonies a day. No one has an actual count, and we could not confirm that Musk made the claim while he led DOGE in 2025.

  • Jesus says to be in the world but not of the world

    Needs Context

    The exact phrase is not in the Bible. It is a summary of John 17:14-18, where Jesus says his followers are not of the world, just as I am not of the world, and that he has sent them into the world, along with John 15:19.

  • In Islam you pray five times a day and follow many rules, and you might get to heaven

    Needs Context

    Five daily prayers are one of the Five Pillars of Islam. Muslims generally teach that salvation ultimately depends on God's mercy rather than on deeds alone, so very rules based is Michael's outside impression rather than how most Muslims describe their faith.

  • In Judaism the law is there to show you that you fall short

    Needs Context

    That reading comes from Paul, who writes in Romans 3:20 that through the law comes knowledge of sin and in Galatians 3:24 that the law was our guardian until Christ came. Jewish tradition generally sees the Torah as a gift and a way of life to be lived in covenant with God, with repentance always available, rather than as a standard meant to show failure.

  • Hume said that amid all your philosophy you should still be a man

    Verified

    In section 1 of An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding (1748), Hume has nature say, Be a philosopher; but, amidst all your philosophy, be still a man.

  • Hume struggled with how we experience red, saying you only experience it in the moment

    Needs Context

    Hume did argue that all ideas, such as the idea of a color, are copies of earlier impressions, and he famously puzzled over the missing shade of blue, a shade one has never seen. Michael's version mixes this with later debates about qualia, but Hume's general point that skeptical philosophy cannot be lived out in daily life is accurate.

  • In Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance the main character goes insane trying to figure out what quality is

    Verified

    Robert Pirsig's 1974 novel is narrated by a man whose earlier self, called Phaedrus, broke down while obsessively pursuing the question of what quality is and was given electroconvulsive therapy. The story draws on Pirsig's own hospitalization in the early 1960s.

  • No solutions, only trade-offs

    Verified

    The line is Thomas Sowell's, usually quoted as There are no solutions. There are only trade-offs. Sowell develops the idea in A Conflict of Visions (1987) and repeats it in later books and interviews.

  • Shorter days mean less sunshine and less vitamin D

    Verified

    Skin makes vitamin D from UVB light, which weakens as the sun sits lower in fall and winter. In Austin, at about 30 degrees north, sun exposure can still produce some vitamin D in winter, but much less than in summer.
