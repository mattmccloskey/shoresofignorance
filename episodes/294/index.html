About This Episode

Matt and Michael open with T.S. Eliot's East Coker III, a dense meditation on darkness, waiting, and the limits of human understanding. The poem's command to wait without hope and wait without love sparks a conversation about the seasons in life and marriage where our old conceptions must die so something new can be born.

The discussion spirals into the nature of second birth. Not just physical birth, but the spiritual rebirth Jesus describes to Nicodemus. They explore how we cannot imagine what we are being born into, how our agency proves insufficient, and why Christianity uniquely promises that God will meet us in the darkness rather than demanding we climb out ourselves.

The episode closes with a reflection on the synoptic Gospels. Why Matthew, Mark, and Luke tell the same stories differently, and what this reveals about truth, perspective, and the impossibility of a single objective account. They also touch on why we must remain humble about the stories we think we know.
