Fact Checks

  • Benjamin Franklin said the problem with advice is that the wise don't need it and the fool won't take it

    Needs Context

    The line is widely attributed to Franklin in the form 'Wise men don't need advice. Fools won't take it,' but there is no clear primary source in Poor Richard or the papers. Matt is loosely quoting a familiar proverb, not a documented Franklin sentence.

  • The oracle tells Socrates he is the wisest man in the land because he knows that he is not wise

    Verified

    In Plato's Apology, the Delphic oracle says no one is wiser than Socrates, and Socrates interprets this as knowing that he does not know.

  • A journal or news outlet asked writers to submit essays on what is wrong with the world, and G.K. Chesterton's response was, 'Dear sirs, I am'

    Needs Context

    The compact Times letter is a much-repeated anecdote for which the American Chesterton Society has found no documentary evidence. A real letter from Chesterton to the Daily News on August 16, 1905, answering 'What is Wrong?', says the answer 'is, or should be, "I am wrong."' The book's title, What's Wrong with the World (1910), is real. Matt already hedges with 'I think it's G.K. Chesterton.'

  • There is a 1 in 36 autism rate

    Verified

    As of this recording in early March 2025, the latest CDC ADDM figure, published in 2023 from 2020 data, was 1 in 36 eight-year-olds. Trump also used 1 in 36 in the March 4 joint address. A newer CDC figure of 1 in 31 was not released until April 2025.

  • 74% of 18-to-24-year-olds are ineligible to join the military

    Incorrect

    The figure still being cited by the Pentagon in 2024 was 77% of 17-to-24-year-olds ineligible without a waiver, from the 2020 Qualified Military Available study, not 74% of 18-to-24-year-olds. An earlier 2013 estimate had been 71%.

  • Annie Duke said the smarter you are, the more biased you are, because you are much better at convincing yourself why what you believe is true

    Verified

    In Thinking in Bets, Duke argues that intelligence can worsen motivated reasoning: smarter or more numerate people are often better at constructing justifications for what they already believe. She draws on work such as Dan Kahan's motivated-numeracy studies.

  • Last night was Trump's State of the Union, or congressional address

    Verified

    On Tuesday, March 4, 2025, Trump delivered his first joint address of the second term. It was not formally a State of the Union, and Matt catches that in the moment. A Wednesday, March 5 recording fits 'last night.'

  • In The Abolition of Man, C.S. Lewis says he does not enjoy the company of small children and recognizes that as a defect in himself

    Verified

    In the first lecture Lewis writes, 'I myself do not enjoy the society of small children: because I speak from within the Tao I recognize this as a defect in myself—just as a man may have to recognize that he is tone deaf or colour blind.' Matt's wording is very close. Calling the book a case against transhumanism is a fair modern reading of the third lecture, though Lewis does not use that word.

  • Hemingway said writing is very easy: all you need to do is open a vein and bleed onto the page, and Buechner opens with that line and then walks through four authors, including Mark Twain, Chesterton, and Shakespeare

    Needs Context

    The book and the King Lear title are right, and the four authors are Gerard Manley Hopkins (the one Matt is blanking on), Twain, Chesterton, and Shakespeare. The bleed-onto-the-page line is not Hemingway's. Quote Investigator traces 'Writing is easy; you just open a vein and bleed' to the sportswriter Red Smith, reported by Walter Winchell in 1949; the Hemingway attribution appears later and is unsupported.

  • Warren Smith is a middle-school teacher for troubled youth whose J.K. Rowling classroom clip went viral after Elon Musk retweeted it, and he has been on Peterson and Rogan

    Needs Context

    The clip, the Musk share, and the Rogan and Peterson appearances are all real (JRE #2261; Peterson EP 504). Smith taught multimedia at a high school for students with behavioral challenges, not a middle school, which is the detail Matt is unsure about in the moment.

  • About 20 senators and representatives read the same script saying the same thing

    Needs Context

    On March 4, 2025, 22 Senate Democrats posted nearly identical videos reading the same anti-Trump script. The show notes say 22 senators. It was senators, not a mix of senators and representatives, and the count is 22 rather than 20.

  • Trump talked about capturing the guy that blew up the 13 Marines in the withdrawal from Afghanistan, and about a kid who survived brain cancer, and half the audience sat silent

    Needs Context

    In the March 4 address Trump did announce the arrest of an ISIS-K planner in the Abbey Gate bombing and did speak about a child who had survived cancer. Abbey Gate killed 13 U.S. service members, 11 of them Marines, not 13 Marines; the man arrested, Mohammad Sharifullah, was described as a planner, not the suicide bomber. Democrats in the chamber largely did not applaud those lines, which is the scene Matt is describing.
