Fact Checks

  • 90% of media organizations in Ukraine are funded by the United States government

    Incorrect

    Matt is repeating a line from the Mike Benz interviews. A related figure, often traced to IMI director Oksana Romaniuk, is that around 90% of Ukrainian media survived the war thanks to grants from a mix of Western donors, including USAID, the EU, and others. That is not the same as 90% of outlets being funded by the U.S. government.

  • When the U.S. shut down the economy in March 2020, only 100 people had died in the entire world of COVID, and John Cullen says it was actually for an asteroid expected to be an extinction-level event

    Incorrect

    By mid-March 2020, when U.S. shutdowns began, worldwide COVID-19 deaths were already in the thousands, not 100, and Italy and China were in crisis. There was no extinction-level asteroid on the way, and the field hospitals were built for the pandemic. The Cullen story is a conspiracy theory that was circulating in 2020 and after; Matt is openly unsure what to make of it.

  • Mike Benz says if a dirty deed is too dirty for the CIA you give it to USAID, because the president has to sign off on CIA covert operations but not on USAID, and judges are blocking him from even looking at the budget

    Needs Context

    Covert action does require a written presidential finding under 50 U.S.C. § 3093. USAID is an appropriated civilian agency, not a covert-action service, so it is not overseen that way, which is the contrast Benz is drawing. 'Too dirty for the CIA' is his characterization, not a legal category. In early February 2025 there were real court fights over DOGE, Treasury, and USAID access and funding freezes; 'not even allowed to look at the budget' overstates those early orders.

  • After 1948 the Western world decided not to invade other countries, and moved instead toward soft-power financial influence

    Needs Context

    The UN Charter of 1945 did constrain wars of conquest, and Perkins describes a postwar turn toward financial leverage through aid and development loans. The date is fuzzy, and the West still fought and intervened often after 1948, including Korea, Vietnam, and the Gulf Wars. Michael is pointing at a real shift in tools, not a literal end to invasion.

  • In Confessions of an Economic Hit Man, 15 to 40% of the loan goes to the actual project, and the rest to payoffs and padding pockets

    Needs Context

    John Perkins has written that the bulk of such loan money is paid to U.S. contractors and in that sense never really leaves the United States; in interviews he has put that share very high, sometimes around 90%. Michael's 15-to-40% 'actual product' figure is a paraphrase of that thesis, not an official statistic, and Perkins's account has long been disputed.

  • 60% of NIH grants go to admin costs, and maybe 40% actually makes it to the study

    Incorrect

    A negotiated NIH facilities-and-administrative rate of around 50-60% is applied to modified total direct costs, not to the whole award, and the administrative portion of that rate has long been capped at 26%. The rest is facilities. A 60% F&A rate typically means something closer to a third of the total award, not 60% of the grant, going to overhead, and it is not all 'admin.' This was a live confusion in the February 2025 fight over NIH indirect costs.

  • We didn't kill bin Laden until 2014 or something

    Incorrect

    Osama bin Laden was killed in the U.S. raid in Abbottabad, Pakistan, on May 2, 2011, Pakistan time, not in 2014. Matt is already hedging with 'or something.'

  • RFK Jr. was a heroin addict, his uncle and father were assassinated while he was a teenager, he got clean in his early or mid-20s, and he has said he began to act as if God was real

    Needs Context

    RFK Jr. has long described a years-long heroin addiction and a recovery that included 12-step work and a deliberate decision to 'act as if' God existed, which matches the story Matt is telling and is close to Jordan Peterson's riff as well. His father was assassinated in 1968, when he was 14. His uncle, President Kennedy, was assassinated in 1963, when he was nine, so 'all while he was a teenager' is slightly off.

  • Thomas Jefferson was 19 when he wrote the Declaration of Independence

    Incorrect

    Jefferson was born April 13, 1743, and the Declaration is 1776, so he was 33. Matt is reaching for the youth of the founding generation to defend Elon's young DOGE staff, and he has the age wrong. (James Monroe was 18 in 1776, but he did not write the Declaration.)

  • I think Benjamin Franklin said fences make good neighbors

    Needs Context

    The familiar line is Robert Frost's, from 'Mending Wall' (1914): 'Good fences make good neighbors.' Franklin does have a cousin of the idea in Poor Richard, 'Love your Neighbor; yet don't pull down your Hedge.' Matt is already hedging with 'I think.'

  • Bill Clinton in the 90s came in and had a balanced budget because he cleaned house, and they fired hundreds of thousands of government employees; Michael thinks this has happened three times, maybe including Truman

    Verified

    The Clinton-Gore National Performance Review reduced the federal civilian workforce by about 377,000 in the 1990s, and the federal budget ran surpluses in the late 1990s. Matt's 'hundreds of thousands' is in the right range, though much of it was attrition and buyouts rather than mass firings. Michael is unsure about the earlier precedent; postwar demobilization under Truman is the usual comparison, and he already says he may have the first one wrong.

  • Elon thinks that he can cut the deficit in half, and Trump thinks that he can get rid of the income tax

    Verified

    As of this February 2025 recording, Musk and DOGE were publicly aiming at on the order of $2 trillion in spending cuts, which would have more than cut a then-roughly-$1.8-trillion deficit in half. Trump had already floated, including in 2024, replacing or eliminating the income tax with tariffs. Matt is describing what they were saying they could do, and he already adds that the claims have 'different degrees of believability.'
