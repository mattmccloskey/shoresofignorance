Fact Checks

  • Where there are no oxen, the manger is clean, but much increase comes from the strength of an ox

    Verified

    Proverbs 14:4, in the ESV: 'Where there are no oxen, the manger is clean, but abundant crops come by the strength of the ox.' Michael's wording is slightly loose ('much increase') and otherwise faithful.

  • The New Testament says the kingdom of heaven is here and now

    Needs Context

    Matt already says he does not know where it is in the Gospels. The closest lines are 'the kingdom of God is at hand' (Mark 1:15; Matthew 4:17) and 'the kingdom of God is in the midst of you' (Luke 17:21). 'Kingdom of heaven' is Matthew's usual phrasing, and 'here and now' is his paraphrase, not a verbatim verse.

  • Israel means those who struggle with God

    Verified

    In Genesis 32:28, Jacob is renamed Israel because he has 'striven with God and with men, and [has] prevailed.' Michael's wording is a fair rendering of the name.

  • The story of Cain and Abel is one of the oldest stories and the first story of real men in the Bible

    Needs Context

    It is the first narrative after the expulsion from Eden that is about grown brothers, sacrifice, and murder (Genesis 4). Calling it the first story of 'real men,' and reading it as resentment toward God rather than jealousy of Abel, is Matt's interpretation. The text itself names both anger and the fallen countenance, and later tradition has often emphasized jealousy.

  • Madeleine L'Engle said stories are great because we can play out different characters and our characters can die instead of us, and Jordan Peterson recapitulates that about ideas dying instead of you

    Needs Context

    Peterson really does say that we think so our ideas can die in our place; it is a recurring riff in Maps of Meaning and in his lectures. A matching, well-attested L'Engle sentence in those words is much harder to pin down. Matt may be remembering a broader L'Engle claim about story as a rehearsal of life and death, then correctly crediting Peterson for the ideas version.

  • It would have taken 286 bitcoin in 2015 to buy Michael's house, and when he sold it the house was worth eight bitcoin

    Needs Context

    This is Michael's recollection of his own house and sale, which we cannot independently price. In 2015 bitcoin traded roughly in the mid hundreds of dollars, so 286 BTC would have been on the order of $70,000 to $130,000 depending on the month, and eight bitcoin would later have been several hundred thousand dollars. The arithmetic can be consistent with a sale years later; it is not something we can verify as a public fact.

  • In Christianity, all fall short before the glory of God

    Verified

    Romans 3:23: 'for all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God.' Michael's 'before the glory of God' is a slight paraphrase of a line he is clearly aiming at.

  • Jesus tells the Pharisees, who claim to see themselves properly, that they are blind, and to the blind he says you will see

    Verified

    In John 9:39-41, Jesus says he came 'that those who do not see may see, and those who see may become blind,' and tells the Pharisees that their claim to see means their guilt remains. Matt compresses that into a confrontation with people who say they already see themselves properly.
