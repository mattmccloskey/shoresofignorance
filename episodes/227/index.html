Fact Checks

  • Nancy Pelosi is worth $230 million, and multiple other members of Congress on both sides have wealth that does not make sense

    Needs Context

    As of this early February 2025 recording, estimates of the Pelosi household's net worth varied widely. Some market trackers later put it in the $230 million range, while disclosure-based estimates such as OpenSecrets's were much lower, around the low hundreds of millions at most and often cited nearer $100 million. Members of Congress on both parties do hold substantial investment wealth, but $230 million was a high-end circulating figure, not a settled official one.

  • There is $177 billion going to Ukraine, and Zelensky says they have only seen $50 billion of that and does not know where the other $120 billion is

    Needs Context

    In a February 2, 2025, Associated Press interview, Zelensky said Ukraine had received about $75-76 billion of roughly $177 billion in U.S. aid, not $50 billion. The remainder was not missing cash: much of the appropriated total was spent in the United States on weapons production, stockpile replacement, training, U.S. forces in Europe, and related programs. His 'I don't know where all the money is' was about that accounting, not a confession that $120 billion had vanished.

  • During the Biden administration, the DoD failed its audit for the sixth year in a row because it literally did not know where the money was

    Needs Context

    The Pentagon's first full department-wide audit was for fiscal year 2018, and it received a disclaimer of opinion every year after that. The result released on November 15, 2024, was the seventh consecutive disclaimer, not the sixth. A disclaimer means auditors could not obtain enough evidence to form an opinion, which is commonly described as a failed audit, but it is not the same as the department literally having no idea where its money was.

  • Elon does not have control of the Treasury

    Needs Context

    Michael is right that Musk did not control the Treasury. What had happened in the days around this recording is that members of DOGE had been granted access to Treasury payment systems, which Treasury later described as read-only, and that access, not formal control, is what Democrats were reacting to.

  • The U.S. debt is 36 or 37 trillion

    Verified

    As of the end of January 2025, total public debt outstanding was about $36.22 trillion. Michael is giving the figure as a range, and it is in the right neighborhood.

  • Thomas Sowell says there are no such things as solutions, there are only trade-offs

    Verified

    Sowell writes in The Vision of the Anointed (1995), 'There are no solutions; there are only trade-offs.' Matt's wording is faithful.

  • Bill Johnson is a pastor at Bethel Church in Reading, California

    Incorrect

    Johnson is a pastor at Bethel Church, but the church is in Redding, California, not Reading.

  • Hydroxychloroquine is super effective against Covid

    Incorrect

    By the time of this recording, large randomized trials, including the UK RECOVERY trial, and WHO guidance had found no meaningful benefit for hydroxychloroquine as a COVID-19 treatment. Trump did promote it in 2020, and that was heavily attacked, but the claim that it turned out to be 'super effective' does not hold.

  • A guy just broke the 24-hour pull-up record at 9,942, with his hands swollen to twice their size and skin ripped off

    Needs Context

    On February 1-2, 2025, days before this recording, Truett Hanes completed 10,001 pull-ups in 24 hours, a new Guinness record, and later interviews showed badly damaged, swollen hands. Matt has the event and the injury right, and the number slightly wrong.

  • Trump said, out of left field, that we're going to own Gaza, the Gaza Strip

    Verified

    On February 4, 2025, the day before this episode's date, Trump said the United States would take over the Gaza Strip and redevelop it, a proposal that immediately dominated the news and is the occasion for the Solomon analogy.

  • From the USAID stuff, there was $8 million for cinnamon-flavored K-Cups in one office where 90 percent of the employees come in one day a month

    Incorrect

    No verified USAID expenditure of $8 million on cinnamon-flavored K-Cups turned up. An $8 million figure circulating in the same week was about federal payments to Politico, mostly not from USAID, and a cinnamon-pod spending claim making the rounds was tied to a satirical account, not an official DOGE finding. Matt is repeating a viral mashup from the first days of the USAID story.

  • In the Gospels, they describe Jesus as Son of Man more times than as Son of God

    Verified

    In the four Gospels, 'Son of Man' is Jesus's most frequent self-designation, appearing on the order of 80 times, well more than 'Son of God.' Matt is recalling a letter from his dad, and the count is right.
