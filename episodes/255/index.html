About This Episode

We compare ourselves both to other people, and to the “inner possibilities” of the life we’re meant to live. In this episode we draw wisdom from Carl Jung and Theodore Roosevelt, and discuss social media, identity, AI, and Cain and Able.

"The world is full of people suffering from the effects of their own unlived life. They become bitter, critical, or rigid, not because the world is cruel to them, but because they have betrayed their own inner possibilities. The artist who never makes art becomes cynical about those who do. The lover who never risks loving mocks romance. The thinker who never commits to a philosophy sneers at belief itself. And yet, all of them suffer, because deep down they know: the life they mock is the life they were meant to live.”
— Carl Jung

“I wish to preach, not the doctrine of ignoble ease, but the doctrine of the strenuous life, the life of toil and effort, of labor and strife; that highest form of success which comes, not to the man who desires mere easy peace, but to the man who does not shrink from danger, from hardship, or from bitter toil.”
— Theodore Roosevelt, 1899

Fact Checks

  • Carl Jung said the world is full of people suffering from the effects of their own unlived life

    Needs Context

    The wording Matt reads is widely circulated online as Jung, often without a primary citation. Jung did write extensively about unlived life, bitterness, and projecting onto others, especially in volumes such as Modern Man in Search of a Soul and the Collected Works, but this polished paragraph is best treated as a popular paraphrase rather than a verified verbatim line from a single Jung text.

  • Bret Weinstein on DarkHorse used Plato's cave to describe algorithmically different realities

    Needs Context

    As of the Oct 2025 recording Matt cited roughly episode 297 and the caves-inside-the-cave analogy. Weinstein and Heying have repeatedly used Plato's cave and feed-fragmentation language on DarkHorse; the exact episode number should be treated as approximate unless checked against that week's catalog.

  • There were No Kings rallies the weekend before this episode

    Verified

    Nationwide No Kings protests against Trump took place on Oct 18, 2025. The episode published Oct 23, 2025, so Michael's this-weekend reference fits the recording window.

  • God tells Moses I am that I am

    Verified

    In Exodus 3:14 God says to Moses, I AM WHO I AM, often rendered I am that I am in older English Bibles.

  • Jesus says my yoke is easy and my burden is light

    Verified

    Matthew 11:30: For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light.

  • In Genesis, Cain kills Abel out of resentment after God prefers Abel's offering

    Verified

    Genesis 4:1-16. After God regards Abel's offering and not Cain's, Cain is angry; God warns him that sin is crouching at the door; Cain kills Abel in the field.

  • Cain goes off and invents weapons of war and becomes a murderous nation

    Needs Context

    Genesis 4 says Cain builds a city and his descendant Tubal-cain forges bronze and iron tools. Later tradition and some interpreters connect Cain's line with violence and weaponry, but the Bible does not say Cain himself invents weapons of war or founds a murderous nation in those exact terms.

  • Elon Musk is worth about $350 billion

    Needs Context

    Musk's net worth fluctuates with Tesla and other holdings. Around mid-to-late 2025 Forbes and Bloomberg estimates often placed him near or above the mid-hundreds of billions at peaks; $350 billion is a ballpark conversational figure, not a fixed audited total.

  • Donald Trump ran again at 78, is a billionaire, and is married to a supermodel

    Needs Context

    Trump was 78 when inaugurated for his second term in January 2025 and is married to Melania Trump, a former model. Net-worth estimates vary widely by source and method; calling him a billionaire matches common public estimates but is not a settled accounting fact.

  • Charlie Kirk said a founding father wrote that the American system would only work for a Christian nation

    Needs Context

    John Adams wrote in 1798 that Our Constitution was made only for a moral and religious people. It is wholly inadequate to the government of any other. Variants about a Christian nation are common in popular Christian political rhetoric, including from Charlie Kirk, but Adams's letter says moral and religious, not specifically Christian nation.

  • Joe Biden said if you're not voting for me, you ain't black

    Verified

    In a May 22, 2020 interview with Charlamagne tha God on The Breakfast Club, Biden said, If you have a problem figuring out whether you're for me or Trump, then you ain't black. He later apologized for the wording.

  • Karine Jean-Pierre put out a book and did a podcast circuit describing herself in intersectional terms

    Verified

    Jean-Pierre's memoir Independent was released in 2025 after she left her White House role, and she did extensive press. She has long described herself as a Black immigrant gay woman; Matt's string of labels matches how she is routinely introduced on that circuit.

  • Theodore Roosevelt said in 1899 that he wished to preach the doctrine of the strenuous life

    Verified

    Roosevelt delivered The Strenuous Life in Chicago on April 10, 1899. The lines Matt reads match the opening of that speech.

  • Older fairy tales like Hansel and Gretel and The Little Mermaid are terrifying compared with many modern children's stories

    Verified

    Andersen's Little Mermaid ends in dissolution and longing, and the Brothers Grimm Hansel and Gretel centers on abandonment and a cannibal witch. Many contemporary children's franchises soften or remove that kind of mortal threat.
